Lesson 1.2: Heat Transfer Processes (熱的傳遞過程)

Heat always flows from hot to cold. But how does it travel? Through a metal spoon, around a pot of soup, and across 150 million km of empty space from the Sun.

Key wordShort meaning
conduction (傳導)Energy passed on by particle collisions (and free electrons); mainly in solids.
convection (對流)Energy carried by the movement of a fluid (liquid or gas).
radiation (輻射)Energy carried by electromagnetic waves (mainly infra-red); needs no medium.
infra-red (IR) (紅外線)Invisible electromagnetic radiation emitted by all objects, more by hotter ones.
Three processesConduction, convection and radiation often happen at the same time. In explanations, name the process that is most important in that situation.

Idea 1

Conduction

Heat one end of a metal rod and soon the other end gets hot: energy travels through the material from the hotter region to the cooler region by conduction. Conduction needs a medium and is most important in solids.

Molecular explanation ⭐Particles in the hotter region vibrate faster (higher average K.E.). They collide with neighbouring slower particles and make them vibrate faster, so energy is passed along from particle to particle.
Why metals are good conductors ⭐Metals have free electrons. They gain energy in the hot region and travel a long way through the metal before colliding, carrying energy quickly to the cooler parts.
Good conductorsPoor conductors (good insulators)
metals: silver, copper, aluminium, ironwood, plastic, polystyrene, cotton wool, glass, water, air
🍳 Kitchen activity: touch testTouch a metal pot, a wooden chopping board and a plastic bowl that have been in the kitchen for hours. The metal feels coldest — but all three are at room temperature! Your hand (≈ 33 °C) is warmer; the metal, a good conductor, takes energy away from your hand faster, so it feels colder.
Common mistakes ⚠️
  • “The metal is colder.” ✗ Same temperature; it just conducts heat away from your hand faster.
  • “A coat keeps us warm because wool is a poor conductor.” Mainly ✗ — it is the trapped air (a very poor conductor) between the fibres that reduces heat loss by conduction (and convection).

Idea 2

Convection

Convection is heat transfer by the movement of the fluid itself. It happens only in liquids and gases, never in solids.

Convection current ⭐When part of a fluid is heated it expands, so its density decreases. The hotter, less dense fluid rises and the cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place. This circulation is a convection current.
  • 🫖 A kettle’s heating element is at the bottom: heated water rises and cold water sinks to be heated.
  • ❄️ An air-conditioner is installed high on the wall: cool air sinks; a heater is placed low: warm air rises.
  • 🕯️ A candle flame points upwards: the hot air rises and cooler air flows in.
  • 🌊 Sea breeze (daytime): the land warms up faster than the sea, air above the land rises and cooler air from the sea flows in. At night the current reverses (land breeze).
🌱 Values: air-conditioning habitsSet the air-conditioner to about 25.5 °C, close windows and doors while it is on, and use a fan to help circulate the air: you save a lot of electricity because less energy has to be transferred.
Idea 3

Radiation

Infra-red radiation ⭐
  • All objects emit infra-red radiation, even ice. The hotter the object, the more radiation it emits (very hot objects also glow with visible light).
  • Radiation is an electromagnetic wave: it needs no medium and can travel through a vacuum (that is how the Sun heats the Earth).
  • All objects also absorb radiation. An object hotter than its surroundings emits more than it absorbs (net emitter), and vice versa.
Factors affecting emission & absorption ⭐temperature of the surface (hotter → emits more) · colour and texture of the surface · surface area (larger → more emission/absorption).
SurfaceEmitterAbsorberReflector
dull blackgoodgoodpoor
shiny / silvery / lightpoorpoorgood

A good emitter is also a good absorber.

🔬 Experiment (Leslie-cube style)Fill two identical cans, one painted dull black and one shiny silver, with the same mass of hot water. Record the temperatures every minute: the black can cools faster. Put both (with cold water) at equal distances from an infra-red lamp: the black can warms up faster.
Common mistake ⚠️“Wrapping food in shiny aluminium foil helps it absorb radiation from the fire.” ✗ Shiny foil is a poor absorber (good reflector) of radiation.

Idea 4

Applications: flasks, greenhouses and solar cookers

🧴 Vacuum flask
FeatureReduces heat transfer by…
vacuum between the double glass wallsconduction and convection (no particles)
silvery coating on the wallsradiation (good reflector, poor emitter)
stopper (plastic, hollow)convection (and evaporation); poor conductor
cork / plastic supportconduction to the outer case
It keeps hot drinks hot and cold drinks cold — it reduces heat transfer in both directions.
🌿 Greenhouse effectGlass lets visible light from the Sun through. Plants and soil absorb it, warm up and emit infra-red, which the glass does not let through easily — the IR is trapped and the greenhouse stays warm for crops. In the atmosphere, greenhouse gases (water vapour, CO₂, CH₄) absorb and re-emit IR in the same way. More CO₂ from burning fuels enhances the effect → global warming, melting polar ice caps, rising sea levels and effects on farming.
☀️ Project: design a solar cooker
  • shiny reflectors direct sunlight onto the pot (good reflectors);
  • pot painted dull black (good absorber);
  • glass/plastic cover: traps IR (greenhouse effect) and stops convection losses;
  • insulated box (e.g. newspaper, polystyrene): poor conductors reduce conduction losses;
  • a pot of water with a large heat capacity heats slowly but stays hot (Lesson 1.3).
Solar (and geothermal) energy are environmentally friendly alternatives to burning fuels.
Answer patterns ⭐ (from the notes)
  • The (object) is a (good/poor) conductor of heat.
  • The hotter (fluid) rises and the cooler (fluid) sinks, forming a convection current.
  • An object with a (shiny / dull black) surface is a (good/poor) (emitter/absorber/reflector) of radiation.
  • It can (reduce/enhance) heat (gain/loss) by (conduction/convection/radiation).
⚠️ Safety: radiation heatersKeep clothes, curtains and papers away from radiant heaters, never dry clothes on them, and switch them off before sleeping or going out.
