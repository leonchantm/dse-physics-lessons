Lesson 1.3: Heat Capacity and Specific Heat Capacity (熱容量和比熱容)

On a sunny day the sand on the beach burns your feet but the sea is still cool. Why do different materials warm up by different amounts when they get the same energy?

Key wordShort meaning
heat capacity C (熱容量)Energy needed to raise the temperature of an object by 1 °C.
specific heat capacity c (比熱容)Energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C.
thermal equilibrium (熱平衡)Objects in contact at the same temperature; no net heat flow.
Constant usedSpecific heat capacity of water c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹. Energy supplied by a heater of power P in time t: E = Pt.

Idea 1

Heat capacity

Definition ⭐Heat capacity = energy transferred ÷ temperature change
C = E/ΔT (the C&A Guide writes C = Q/ΔT)  ⇒  E = CΔT
Unit: J °C⁻¹ (or J K⁻¹ — a change of 1 °C is the same as a change of 1 K)

A cup of water and a swimming pool are both water, but the pool needs far more energy for the same temperature rise: it has a much larger heat capacity. Heat capacity belongs to an object and depends on its mass and material.

✏️ Example (from the notes)A slice of bread needs 6000 J to warm from 25 °C to 40 °C.
(a) C = 6000 ÷ (40 − 25) = 400 J °C⁻¹
(b) A loaf of 8 such slices: C = 8 × 400 = 3200 J °C⁻¹
Idea 2

Specific heat capacity

Definition ⭐Specific heat capacity = energy transferred ÷ (mass × temperature change)
c = E/(mΔT) (guide: c = Q/(mΔT))  ⇒  E = mcΔT
Unit: J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹ (or J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹)
Link: C = mc
  • c depends only on the material, not on the mass.
  • Capital C = heat capacity (object); small c = specific heat capacity (substance).
  • A substance with a small c warms up (and cools down) quickly.

Worked examples

Solving heating problems

Strategy ⭐1. Energy supplied by a heater: E = Pt (t in seconds!). 2. Energy for the temperature change: E = mcΔT. 3. Use conservation of energy: energy supplied = energy absorbed + energy lost.
✏️ Example 1: kettleA 1200 W kettle heats 1.5 kg of water from 25 °C to 100 °C. Assume no energy loss.
E = mcΔT = 1.5 × 4200 × (100 − 25) = 472 500 J
Pt = E ⇒ t = 472 500 ÷ 1200 = 394 s (≈ 6.6 min)
✏️ Example 2: with energy lossA 300 W heater is switched on for 4 minutes in 0.80 kg of a liquid (c = 2500 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹) at 20 °C. 25% of the energy is lost to the surroundings. Find the final temperature.
Energy supplied = 300 × 240 = 72 000 J
Energy absorbed = 75% × 72 000 = 54 000 J
ΔT = 54 000 ÷ (0.80 × 2500) = 27 °C ⇒ final temperature = 47 °C
📏 Check with units (dimension analysis)ΔT = E/(mc) has units J ÷ (kg × J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹) = °C ✓. If your units do not work out, the formula has been rearranged wrongly.
Common mistakes ⚠️
  • Using minutes instead of seconds in E = Pt.
  • Using the final temperature instead of the temperature change ΔT.
  • Using g instead of kg.
Practice 1

Practice: heat capacity and specific heat capacity

Experiment

Determining the specific heat capacity of a substance

🔬 Aluminium block (electrical method)
  1. Weigh the block (m). Insert an immersion heater and a thermometer into its holes, adding a few drops of oil for good thermal contact.
  2. Wrap the block in cotton wool and stand it on a wooden block (poor conductors → less heat loss).
  3. Record the initial temperature and joulemeter reading.
  4. Heat until the temperature has risen by about 10 °C, then switch off.
  5. Wait and record the highest temperature and the final joulemeter reading.
c = (Ef − Ei) ÷ [m(Tf − Ti)]
🔬 Water or another liquidUse an immersion heater in water in a polystyrene cup with a lid; stir gently so the temperature is uniform; record the highest temperature after switching off.
✏️ Sample resultsm = 1.0 kg; joulemeter 12 450 J → 23 820 J; temperature 22.0 °C → 33.5 °C
c = 11 370 ÷ (1.0 × 11.5) = 989 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹ (standard value: 900)
Is the result too high or too low? ⭐Some of the energy supplied is lost to the surroundings (and used to heat the thermometer and the heater). The energy used in c = E/(mΔT) is larger than the energy actually absorbed by the block, so the measured c is higher than the true value.
Exam tip: always say which quantity is affected and in which direction.
  • Why raise the temperature by only about 10 °C? A bigger temperature difference from the room → more heat loss.
  • Why wait for the highest temperature? The heater is hotter than the block and keeps giving it energy after it is switched off.

Idea 3

Mixing hot and cold: conservation of energy

When a hot object and a cold object are put in contact, heat flows from hot to cold until they reach the same temperature (thermal equilibrium). If no energy is lost to the surroundings:

Energy lost by the hot object = energy gained by the cold object ⭐mhch(Th − T) = mccc(T − Tc)
T = final common temperature
If the temperature of an object…it…
risesabsorbs energy: E = mc(T − Tc)
fallsreleases energy: E = mc(Th − T)
✏️ Example 10.20 kg of water at 90 °C is mixed with 0.30 kg of water at 20 °C.
0.20 × 4200 × (90 − T) = 0.30 × 4200 × (T − 20)
18 − 0.2T = 0.3T − 6 ⇒ T = 48 °C
✏️ Example 2 (method of mixtures)A 0.50 kg copper block (c = 390 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹) at 100 °C is put into 0.20 kg of water at 20 °C in a polystyrene cup.
0.50 × 390 × (100 − T) = 0.20 × 4200 × (T − 20)
195(100 − T) = 840(T − 20) ⇒ T = 36 300 ÷ 1035 = 35.1 °C
Common mistake ⚠️Writing “the hot object gives its temperature to the cold one”. Energy is transferred, not temperature; the final temperature is nearer to that of the object with the larger heat capacity (mc).
Try it!

Simulation: heating and mixing lab

Heater mode: heat a sample with an electric heater and watch the temperature–time graph. Mixing mode: drop the sample, taken from boiling water (100 °C), into a cup of water at 20 °C. (1 s on screen = 30 s.)

Things to try
  • Heater mode: same mass and power, compare water and copper. Which graph is steeper? (gradient = P/(mc))
  • Double the mass: the gradient halves.
  • Add 20% energy loss: the temperature rise is only 80% as big — an experiment would then give a value of c that is too high.
  • Mixing mode: the final temperature is closer to the starting temperature of the object with the larger heat capacity.

Idea 4

Why the high specific heat capacity of water matters

Water has a very high specific heat capacity (4200 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹): it absorbs a lot of energy for a small temperature rise, and gives out a lot of energy as it cools.

  • 🚗 Coolant: water carries heat away from car engines, power stations and large air-conditioning systems — power stations are often built by the sea.
  • 🏙️ Climate: the sea warms up and cools down slowly, so coastal cities like Hong Kong have a small temperature range between day and night (and summer and winter). Inland deserts, with sand of low c, have very hot days and cold nights.
  • 🧍 Body temperature: our bodies are about 70% water, which helps to keep body temperature steady.
  • 🛌 Hot-water bottles stay warm for a long time.
🌱 Values: saving energy at homeHeating water uses a lot of energy because c is large. Boil only the amount of water you need, keep the kettle free of scale, and use a lid when cooking — this saves electricity and money.
