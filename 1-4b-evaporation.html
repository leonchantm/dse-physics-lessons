Lesson 1.4b: Evaporation (蒸發)

Wet clothes dry on a bamboo pole at 30 °C, far below 100 °C. Sweat cools you down — but not on a humid Hong Kong summer day. Why?

Key wordShort meaning
evaporation (蒸發)Liquid → gas at the surface, at any temperature.
humidity (濕度)How much water vapour is already in the air.
cooling effect (冷卻效應)Evaporation takes energy (latent heat) from the remaining liquid and its surroundings.
Builds onLatent heat of vaporization (Lesson 1.4a) and temperature as average K.E. (Lesson 1.1).

Idea 1

Evaporation happens below the boiling point

A puddle dries up and wet hair dries without being heated to 100 °C. A liquid can change into a gas by evaporation at any temperature.

BoilingEvaporation
only at the boiling pointat any temperature
throughout the liquidat the surface only
bubbles of vapour formno bubbles
Both need the latent heat of vaporization.
Common mistakes ⚠️
  • “Evaporation does not need energy.” ✗ It needs latent heat just like boiling — that is why it cools things.
  • “Water only turns into vapour at 100 °C.” ✗ That is boiling. Evaporation happens at room temperature too.
Idea 2

Evaporation in terms of molecular motion

Explanation ⭐
  • Molecules in a liquid move randomly with different speeds; they keep colliding and exchanging energy.
  • Some molecules near the surface gain enough K.E. to overcome the attraction of the other molecules and escape into the air.
  • The escaping molecules are the faster ones, so the average K.E. of the molecules left behind decreases → the temperature of the liquid falls.

Fast molecules deep inside usually hit other molecules and lose energy before reaching the surface — that is why evaporation happens only at the surface.

Idea 3

The cooling effect of evaporation

Cooling effect ⭐An evaporating liquid needs latent heat of vaporization. It takes this energy from the remaining liquid and from the object it is on (e.g. your skin), so these are cooled.
  • 💦 Sweating: sweat evaporates from the skin, taking latent heat from the body.
  • 🧴 An alcohol swab on the arm feels cold: alcohol evaporates quickly, absorbing energy from the skin.
  • 🏊 Leaving a swimming pool on a windy day, you feel cold: the water on your skin evaporates quickly.
  • 🏺 Water in an unglazed clay pot stays cool as water seeping through evaporates.
The reverse: condensation heatsWhen vapour condenses on a surface it releases latent heat. Steaming food cooks it this way.
🥵 Heat stroke — reading & discussionOn hot, humid days sweat evaporates slowly, so the body cannot lose heat effectively; body temperature can rise above 40 °C (heat stroke, a medical emergency).
Precautions: drink plenty of water, wear loose light-coloured clothing, avoid strenuous exercise under strong sun, rest in shade; watch for the Very Hot Weather Warning.
Care: move the person to a cool place, loosen clothing, sponge with water and fan them (increases evaporation), and call 999.

Idea 4

Factors affecting the rate of evaporation

FactorFaster evaporation when…Molecular reason
temperature of the liquidhigherhigher average K.E. → more molecules have enough energy to escape
surface arealargerevaporation happens only at the surface → more molecules at the surface
humidity of the airlowerfewer vapour molecules above the liquid → fewer return to the liquid
air movement (wind)windierescaped vapour molecules are blown away, so fewer return
🔬 Investigation: a fair testPut equal masses of water in dishes on electronic balances and record the mass every 10 minutes. Change one factor only (e.g. dish area, or a fan on/off), keeping the others the same (same starting temperature, same room, same initial mass). The rate of mass loss measures the rate of evaporation.

Worked example

How much does sweating cool you?

✏️ ExampleA 60 kg runner evaporates 1.2 kg of sweat in 1.0 hour. Take ℓv of water at body temperature = 2.43 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹ and the specific heat capacity of the body = 3470 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹.
(a) Average rate of heat loss by evaporation = mℓv/t = 1.2 × 2.43 × 10⁶ ÷ 3600 = 810 W
(b) If this energy could not escape, how long would it take to raise the body temperature from 37 °C to 40 °C?
E = mcΔT = 60 × 3470 × 3 = 624 600 J ⇒ t = 624 600 ÷ 810 = 771 s (about 13 minutes!)
Notes ⚠️ℓv at body temperature (2.43 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹) is a little larger than at 100 °C (2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹). Use the value given in the question.
