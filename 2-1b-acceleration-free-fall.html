Lesson 2.1b: Acceleration and Free Fall (加速度與自由落體)

Things speed up, slow down and fall. In this lesson you learn acceleration, the four equations of motion, and how things fall.

Key wordShort meaning
acceleration (加速度)How fast the velocity changes. Unit m s⁻².
uniformly accelerated motion (勻加速運動)Motion with a constant acceleration.
free fall (自由落體)Falling under gravity only (no air resistance).
acceleration due to gravity, g (重力加速度)g = 9.81 m s⁻², downward.
air resistance (空氣阻力)A push from the air against the motion.
terminal velocity (終端速度)The top steady speed of a falling object in air.

Idea 1

Acceleration (加速度)

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity (速度的變化率).

Formulaa = (v − u) ÷ t
u = initial velocity (初速度), v = final velocity (末速度), t = time taken

Unit: m s⁻². a = 2 m s⁻² means the velocity changes by 2 m/s every second.

Acceleration is a vector.

🚆 MTR train leaving a stationFrom rest (u = 0) to 20 m s⁻¹ in 20 s: a = (20 − 0) ÷ 20 = 1 m s⁻².
🚗 Car brakingFrom 15 m s⁻¹ to 0 in 3 s: a = (0 − 15) ÷ 3 = −5 m s⁻². The minus sign means the acceleration is opposite to the motion: the car slows down (deceleration (減速)).
RememberConstant velocity → acceleration = 0.
Idea 2

Finding acceleration from a v–t graph

Rule ⭐Gradient (斜率) of the v–t graph = acceleration. A straight line means the acceleration is constant (uniformly accelerated motion).
✏️ Worked example
  1. Pick two points: (0 s, 2 m/s) and (4 s, 10 m/s).
  2. Gradient = Δv ÷ Δt = (10 − 2) ÷ (4 − 0)
  3. = 8 ÷ 4 = 2 m s⁻²

The a–t graph for the same motion: a horizontal line at a = 2 m s⁻².

Line sloping down: negative gradient → slowing down. Here a = (0 − 12) ÷ 6 = −2 m s⁻².

Idea 3

Deriving the four equations (推導運動方程)

For uniformly accelerated motion in a straight line, the v–t graph is a straight line. The area under it is a trapezium (梯形).

1Gradient = acceleration: a = (v − u) ÷ t
2Multiply both sides by t: at = v − u, so
① v = u + at
3Area = displacement. Trapezium area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × width:
② s = ½(u + v)t
4Put ① into ②: s = ½(u + u + at)t = ½(2u + at)t, so
③ s = ut + ½at²
(Check: rectangle ut + triangle ½ × t × at ✓)
5From ①: t = (v − u) ÷ a. Put into ②: s = ½(u + v)(v − u) ÷ a = (v² − u²) ÷ 2a
6Multiply both sides by 2a: 2as = v² − u², so
④ v² = u² + 2as
The four equations (constant a only!)
EquationDoes NOT contain
① v = u + ats
② s = ½(u + v)ta
③ s = ut + ½at²v
④ v² = u² + 2ast
Idea 4

Worked examples: which equation?

Method ⭐ ① List what you know (u, v, a, s, t). ② Write what you want. ③ Find the quantity you do not know and do not want. ④ Choose the equation without it.
✏️ ① v = u + atAn MTR train starts from rest with a = 1.2 m s⁻² for 15 s. Find v.
  1. Know: u = 0, a = 1.2, t = 15. Want: v. (No s.)
  2. v = 0 + (1.2)(15)
  3. v = 18 m s⁻¹
✏️ ② s = ½(u + v)tA car speeds up from 10 to 20 m s⁻¹ in 5 s. Find s.
  1. Know: u = 10, v = 20, t = 5. Want: s. (No a.)
  2. s = ½(10 + 20)(5)
  3. s = 75 m
✏️ ③ s = ut + ½at²u = 4 m s⁻¹, a = 2 m s⁻², t = 3 s. Find s.
  1. Know: u, a, t. Want: s. (No v.)
  2. s = (4)(3) + ½(2)(3²)
  3. s = 12 + 9 = 21 m
✏️ ④ v² = u² + 2asAn MTR train brakes from 20 m s⁻¹ to rest in 100 m. Find a.
  1. Know: u = 20, v = 0, s = 100. Want: a. (No t.)
  2. 0² = 20² + 2a(100) → 0 = 400 + 200a
  3. a = −400 ÷ 200 = −2 m s⁻² (slowing down)
✏️ When t is unknownA car has u = 5 m s⁻¹ and a = 2 m s⁻². How long does it take to go 24 m?
  1. Step 1 — find v first with ④: v² = 5² + 2(2)(24) = 25 + 96 = 121, so v = 11 m s⁻¹
  2. Step 2 — find t with ①: 11 = 5 + 2t → t = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 s
  3. Check with ③: s = 5(3) + ½(2)(3²) = 15 + 9 = 24 m ✓
Tips
  • "From rest" (由靜止開始) → u = 0. "Comes to rest" → v = 0.
  • Choose a + direction. Slowing down → a is negative.
  • Always write the unit.

Idea 5

Free fall (自由落體) and g

Acceleration due to gravity (重力加速度) Without air resistance, all objects fall with the same acceleration, whatever their mass:
g = 9.81 m s⁻², downward.

Some questions use g = 10 m s⁻² to make the numbers easy. Use the value the question gives you.

SignsChoose a + direction. If up = +, then a = −9.81 m s⁻². For a dropped object you may take down = +, then a = +9.81 m s⁻².
In a vacuum, the feather and the coin fall together.
🪶 The feather-and-guinea ideaIn air, a feather falls slowly because of air resistance. When the air is pumped out of the tube, the feather and the coin (a "guinea") hit the bottom at the same time. Galileo said that, without air, heavy and light objects fall together.
✏️ Worked example (take down = +, g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A stone is dropped from rest from a bridge. It hits the water after 2.0 s.
  1. Know: u = 0, a = 9.81, t = 2.0
  2. Speed: v = u + at = 0 + (9.81)(2.0) = 19.6 m s⁻¹
  3. Height: s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½(9.81)(2.0²) = 19.6 m
Idea 6

Measuring g in the lab

Method 1: Ticker tape (紙帶) (50 Hz)

A weight falls and pulls a tape through a ticker-tape timer. We cut the tape into 5-tick sections (each 0.1 s).

Section1234
Length / cm4.914.724.534.3
v = length ÷ 0.1 s0.49 m/s1.47 m/s2.45 m/s3.43 m/s
time at middle of section0.05 s0.15 s0.25 s0.35 s
✏️ g from the gradient
  1. Plot v against t: a straight line.
  2. g = gradient = Δv ÷ Δt = (3.43 − 0.49) ÷ (0.35 − 0.05)
  3. = 2.94 ÷ 0.30 = 9.8 m s⁻²

Method 2: Light gates (光閘) and a data logger

A card falls through two light gates. The data logger measures the speed at each gate and the time between them.

✏️ Examplev₁ = 1.00 m s⁻¹, v₂ = 3.94 m s⁻¹, time between gates = 0.30 s.
g = (v₂ − v₁) ÷ t = 2.94 ÷ 0.30 = 9.8 m s⁻²
Why is our answer a little less than 9.81?Friction on the tape and air resistance slow the object a little. Every experiment has some uncertainty (不確定性). Repeat and take an average.

Idea 7

A ball thrown up: graphs and the "top" ⚾

A ball is thrown up at 20 m s⁻¹. Take up = + and g = 10 m s⁻² (easy numbers), so a = −10 m s⁻².

s–t: a curve. Highest point at t = 2 s.

v–t: a straight line, from +20 down to −20. It crosses 0 at the top.

a–t: a horizontal line at −10, all the time.

✏️ Using the equations (u = +20, a = −10)
  1. Time to the top (v = 0): 0 = 20 + (−10)t → t = 2 s
  2. Maximum height: v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 20² + 2(−10)s → s = 400 ÷ 20 = 20 m
  3. Back at the hand (s = 0): t = 4 s, v = 20 + (−10)(4) = −20 m s⁻¹ (same speed, now downward)
⚠ Common mistake: "At the top, the acceleration is zero." Wrong! At the top, the velocity is 0 for an instant, but gravity still pulls down. The acceleration is still 9.81 m s⁻² downward (−9.81 with up = +). The velocity is still changing: from up to down. If a were 0, the ball would stay at the top forever!
Idea 8

Air resistance (空氣阻力) and terminal velocity (終端速度)

  • Air resistance acts against the motion.
  • The faster the object, the bigger the air resistance.
  • A falling object speeds up, so air resistance grows. The acceleration gets smaller and smaller.
  • When air resistance becomes as big as the weight, the acceleration becomes zero. The object falls at a constant terminal velocity.
🪂 ExamplesA sheet of paper reaches terminal velocity very quickly. A skydiver reaches about 55 m s⁻¹. Opening a parachute increases air resistance, so the terminal velocity drops to about 5 m s⁻¹ for a safe landing.

v–t graph of a falling object (down = +). Dashed: no air resistance (straight line, gradient g). Solid: with air resistance (gradient gets smaller until v is constant).

🏢 Danger: objects falling from high-rise buildings (高空墮物) An object falls from the 30th floor (about 90 m). Without air resistance:
v² = u² + 2as = 0 + 2(9.81)(90) ≈ 1766, so v ≈ 42 m s⁻¹ (about 150 km/h)!
Air resistance lowers this a little, but a flower pot, a glass bottle or a tool still hits the ground very fast. It can seriously injure or kill someone.
  • Never throw anything out of a window.
  • Do not leave flower pots or objects on window ledges.
  • People who throw objects from buildings can be prosecuted.
