Idea 1

What is a force? (力)

A force is a push or a pull. A force can start, stop, speed up, slow down or turn a moving object, or change its shape.

  • Force is a vector: it has a size and a direction. We draw it as an arrow.
  • Unit: the newton (N) (牛頓). (We define 1 N exactly in Lesson 2.2c.)
  • We measure forces with a spring balance (彈簧秤) or a force sensor (力感應器).
Net force (淨力)When several forces act, the net force is their total (vector sum). Forces in opposite directions subtract.
Example: 10 N right and 4 N left → net force = 6 N right.
Common forces: weight (重量), normal reaction (法向反作用力), friction (摩擦力), tension (張力), air resistance (空氣阻力).
Idea 2

Inertia (慣性) and mass (質量)

InertiaEvery object tends to keep doing what it is doing: if it is at rest, it stays at rest; if it is moving, it keeps moving at the same velocity. This tendency is called inertia.
🚌 Bus braking suddenlyYour body was moving forward with the bus. When the bus stops, your body tends to keep moving forward, so you lean forward. When the bus starts suddenly, your body tends to stay at rest, so you lean backward.
🪙 Coin on a cardPut a coin on a card on top of a cup. Flick the card away quickly. The coin tends to stay at rest and drops into the cup. (The same idea: pulling a tablecloth quickly from under the dishes.)
Inertia and mass ⭐The larger the mass, the larger the inertia. Mass is a measure of inertia.
A full supermarket trolley is harder to start moving, and harder to stop, than an empty one. A loaded lorry needs a much longer distance to stop than a car.
🚗 Car safety: seatbelts (安全帶)In a crash the car stops suddenly, but because of inertia the passengers keep moving forward at the car’s old speed and can hit the windscreen. A seatbelt provides the force to stop them with the car. That is why the law in Hong Kong requires drivers and passengers (including in taxis, minibuses and coaches with seatbelts) to wear them.
💺 Head rests (頭枕)When a car is hit from behind and suddenly moves forward, your head tends to stay at rest (inertia), so your neck bends backward. The head rest pushes your head forward with the car and prevents neck injury.

Idea 3

Newton’s First Law of motion (牛頓第一運動定律)

Newton’s First LawEvery object keeps its state of rest or constant velocity (uniform motion) unless a net force acts on it.
Concept key ⭐constant velocity (v = constant) ⇔ no acceleration (a = 0) ⇔ no net force (Fnet = 0)

So: net force = 0 ⇔ at rest or uniform motion (a = 0). A moving object does not need a force to keep moving!

SituationForcesNet forceMotion
📕 A book on a tableWeight W down = normal reaction R up0stays at rest
🚗 A car at a steady 60 km/h on a straight roadDriving force forward = friction + air resistance backward0uniform motion
🪂 A skydiver at terminal velocity (Lesson 2.1b)Air resistance up = weight down0uniform motion
🏒 An air-hockey puck gliding on the table(almost) no friction; W balanced by the air cushion0keeps moving at constant velocity until it hits something
🛰️ A space probe far from any star(almost) no force at all0keeps moving at constant velocity without an engine
🧑‍🔬 Aristotle vs GalileoAristotle (ancient Greece) believed a push is needed to keep an object moving — that is what we feel every day. Galileo doubted this. A ball rolls down one ramp and up another. It rises to almost the same height. Make the second ramp less steep: it rolls further. Make it flat: with no friction it would roll for ever. Galileo (about 1600) saw that friction, not a “lack of force”, stops things. Newton (1687) wrote this as his First Law. Their ideas changed science.
⚠ Common mistake“A moving object must have a forward force on it.” Wrong! If the net force is zero, a moving object keeps moving at constant velocity. A force is needed only to change the velocity.
Idea 4

Friction (摩擦力)

FrictionFriction acts along the surfaces in contact. It opposes motion, or the tendency of motion (when something is about to slide but has not started yet).

Pushing a heavy box: three stages

  1. Small push, box does not move. Friction is equal and opposite to the push (net force 0). This is friction opposing the tendency of motion.
  2. Push harder. Friction grows too, up to a maximum value.
  3. Push more than the maximum. The box starts to slide. Friction now stays about constant (a little smaller than the maximum) and acts against the motion.

Friction against the applied push. Before sliding, friction = push (sloping line). After sliding starts, friction is roughly constant.

👍 Friction is useful
  • Walking: your foot pushes backward on the ground; friction on your foot acts forward.
  • Tyre treads (輪胎坑紋) and brakes need friction. On wet roads tyre grooves push water away so friction is not lost.
👎 Reducing friction
  • Oil (lubricant), ball bearings, smooth surfaces.
  • Air tracks / ice: almost no friction, so a moving glider keeps moving at almost constant velocity — Newton’s First Law in the lab!

Things to try
  • Wooden floor, 5 kg, push 10 N: the box does not move. Friction = 10 N (opposing the tendency of motion). Increase the push slowly until it slides.
  • Air track: push, then ✋ Remove push. Net force = 0, so the box keeps moving at constant velocity (First Law).
  • Wooden floor: start it sliding, then press ⚖. Push = friction, net force = 0, so v stays constant.
  • Remove the push on wood or rubber: friction is the only horizontal force, so the box slows down and stops.
  • Compare 2 kg and 10 kg with the same push on the air track: the bigger mass (more inertia) gains speed more slowly.
