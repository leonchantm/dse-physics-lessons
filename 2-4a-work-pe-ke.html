Lesson 2.4a: Work, P.E. and K.E. (功、勢能和動能)

Lifting a bag, pulling a suitcase, speeding up a car — each needs energy. Doing work is how a force transfers that energy.

Key wordShort meaning
work done (功), WEnergy transferred by a force when the object moves. Unit: joule (J) = N m.
gravitational potential energy (重力勢能), P.E.Energy an object has because of its position under gravity.
kinetic energy (動能), K.E.Energy an object has because of its motion.
reference level (參考水平)The level where we take P.E. = 0.
Energy is a scalarWork, P.E. and K.E. have size only (no direction). They are all measured in joules (J).

Idea 1

Mechanical work is a way of transferring energy

When a force moves an object in the direction of the force, the force does work and energy is transferred.

Work done (force along the motion)W = F s
W: work done (J), F: force (N), s: displacement in the direction of the force (m)
1 J = 1 N m: the work done when a 1 N force moves an object 1 m.
  • You push a trolley: chemical energy in your body → K.E. of the trolley (and heat).
  • You lift a bag: chemical energy → P.E. of the bag.
  • Work done on an object = energy gained by it (if no energy is lost).
No movement or no force along the motion → no work
  • Pushing hard on a wall that does not move: s = 0, so W = 0 (you feel tired, but no work is done on the wall).
  • The normal reaction on a block sliding on a floor is perpendicular to the motion: it does no work.
  • Carrying a bag while walking at constant velocity on level ground: your upward force on the bag is perpendicular to the motion: no work on the bag.
Idea 2

Force at an angle: W = Fs cos θ

Definition ⭐W = F s cos θ
θ = angle between the force F and the displacement s.
Only the component along the displacement (F cos θ) does work.
θcos θwork done by F
0°1W = Fs (maximum)
90°0W = 0
180°−1W = −Fs (e.g. friction)
✏️ Pulling a suitcaseA man pulls a suitcase with a force of 50 N at 30° above the horizontal for 5 m along the ground. Friction is 20 N.
  1. Work done by the pulling force: W = Fs cos θ = 50 × 5 × cos 30° = 217 J (216.5 J)
  2. Work done against friction = 20 × 5 = 100 J (this energy becomes heat / internal energy)
  3. Energy gained by the suitcase as K.E. = 217 − 100 = 117 J
Common mistakes ⚠️Using sin θ instead of cos θ. Draw the force and the displacement; θ is the angle between them. Also, s must be the displacement while the force acts.

Idea 3

Gravitational potential energy: P.E. = mgh

Gravitational potential energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its position under gravity. Raise an object higher → more P.E.

DerivationLift a mass m slowly at constant velocity through a height h.
Net force = 0, so the lifting force F = weight = mg.
Work done by F = F × h = mgh.
This work is stored as P.E., so P.E. = mgh

h is the height above the reference level (where P.E. = 0). We may choose any level (usually the ground), but we must state it. Only the change in P.E., mgΔh, matters in problems.

✏️ Example (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A 2.0 kg book is lifted from the floor onto a shelf 1.5 m high.
Gain in P.E. = mgh = 2.0 × 9.81 × 1.5 = 29.4 J (= minimum work done by you).
Taking the shelf as the reference level instead, the book’s P.E. on the floor is −29.4 J and on the shelf is 0 — the change is still 29.4 J.
Idea 4

Kinetic energy: K.E. = ½mv²

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion. A faster or heavier object has more K.E.

DerivationA constant net force F acts on a mass m, at rest, on a smooth surface. It moves s and reaches speed v.
From v² = u² + 2as with u = 0: s = v² ÷ (2a)
Newton’s 2nd law: F = ma
Work done W = Fs = ma × v²/(2a) = ½mv²
All the work becomes K.E., so K.E. = ½mv²
  • K.E. ∝ m (double mass → double K.E.)
  • K.E. ∝ v² (double speed → 4 times K.E.) — this is why speeding is so dangerous.
  • K.E. is never negative.
✏️ ExampleA 0.06 kg tennis ball is served at 50 m s⁻¹.
K.E. = ½ × 0.06 × 50² = 75 J.
Speed from K.E.: v = √(2 K.E. ÷ m).
Net work = change in K.E.If other forces (e.g. friction) also act, the K.E. gained = work done by the net force = work done by the applied force − work done against friction. You can check this in the simulation.
Activity

Estimating work and kinetic energy

Physicists often make quick order-of-magnitude estimates. Choose sensible values, round them, and calculate.

TaskEstimate (g ≈ 10 m s⁻²)
📕 Lift a book onto a desk1 kg, 1 m: W = mgh ≈ 1 × 10 × 1 = 10 J
🪀 Stretch a spring exerciseraverage force ≈ 100 N over 0.3 m: W ≈ 100 × 0.3 = 30 J
🏢 Climb 10 floors of stairs60 kg, 30 m: W ≈ 60 × 10 × 30 ≈ 2 × 10⁴ J
⛰️ Climb Lantau Peak (934 m)60 kg: W ≈ 60 × 10 × 934 ≈ 6 × 10⁵ J
Moving objectEstimate K.E. = ½mv²
🚗 Speeding car1500 kg, 30 m s⁻¹ (108 km/h): ≈ 7 × 10⁵ J
🏃 Sprinter70 kg, 10 m s⁻¹: ≈ 3.5 × 10³ J
⚽ Kicked football0.45 kg, 25 m s⁻¹: ≈ 140 J
💨 Air molecule (N₂)4.7 × 10⁻²⁶ kg, 500 m s⁻¹: ≈ 6 × 10⁻²¹ J
NoteThe stair and Lantau Peak values are the minimum work against gravity (gain in P.E.). Your body actually uses much more energy, because it is far from 100% efficient. The spring uses average force × extension, because the force grows as the spring stretches.

Simulation: work → energy

Pull mode: a force F at angle θ pulls a block 10 m along a floor with friction. Compare the work done with the K.E. gained. Lift mode: lift a mass at constant velocity and compare the work done with the P.E. gained.

Things to try
  • Set friction to 0 and θ = 0: work done by F = K.E. gained exactly (this is how we derived K.E. = ½mv²).
  • Increase θ with the same F: less work is done (F cos θ is smaller).
  • Lift mode: double h or double m and watch the P.E. double.
