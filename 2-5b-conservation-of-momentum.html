Lesson 2.5b: Conservation of Momentum (動量守恆)

When snooker balls collide, cars crash or a rifle fires, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after. This lesson shows why — and how to use it.

Key wordShort meaning
law of conservation of momentum (動量守恆定律)Total momentum stays constant if no net external force acts.
elastic collision (彈性碰撞)Total K.E. is conserved.
inelastic collision (非彈性碰撞)Some K.E. is lost (to heat, sound, deformation).
perfectly inelastic collision (完全非彈性碰撞)The objects stick together; the K.E. loss is the greatest.
explosion (爆炸)A system at rest separates into parts.

Idea 1

The law of conservation of momentum

Law ⭐The total momentum of a system is conserved (stays constant) provided that no net external force acts on the system.
mAuA + mBuB = mAvA + mBvB

🧪 Experiment: on an air track (or with dynamics trolleys / pucks on an air table) the friction is very small. Light gates or a motion sensor measure the velocities before and after a collision.

Calculate Σmu before and Σmv after. They agree (within experimental error) for every kind of collision.

RememberMomentum is a vector: choose a positive direction and use + and − signs.
Idea 2

Why? Newton’s Third Law

DerivationDuring the collision, A pushes B and B pushes A.
Newton’s 3rd law: FBA = −FAB (force on B by A, force on A by B)
They act for the same time t.
FBAt = −FABt ⇒ ΔpB = −ΔpA
⇒ ΔpA + ΔpB = 0: the total momentum does not change.

Whatever momentum A loses, B gains. This only works if there is no external net force (e.g. large friction would change the total).

Conservation law vs Newton’s Second LawTo use F = ma for a collision, we would need to know how the force changes during the very short impact — usually impossible. The conservation law only needs the velocities before and after. That is why conservation laws are so powerful.
Examples

Solving 1-D momentum problems

Method ✍️1. Draw “before” and “after” sketches. 2. Choose a positive direction. 3. Write Σmu = Σmv. 4. Solve, and give the direction.
✏️ Trolleys stick togetherA 2 kg trolley moving at 3 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 1 kg trolley. They stick together.
(2)(3) + (1)(0) = (2 + 1)v
v = 6 ÷ 3 = 2 m s⁻¹ in the original direction.
✏️ Head-on collisionA 1 kg cart at 4 m s⁻¹ (right) meets a 2 kg cart at 1 m s⁻¹ (left). They stick. Right = +.
(1)(4) + (2)(−1) = (3)v
v = 2 ÷ 3 = 0.67 m s⁻¹ to the right.
✏️ Explosion: rifle recoilA 3 kg rifle at rest fires a 0.01 kg bullet at 600 m s⁻¹. Total momentum before = 0.
0 = (0.01)(600) + (3)v
v = −2 m s⁻¹: the rifle recoils at 2 m s⁻¹ backwards.
✏️ One moves onA 0.5 kg glider at 0.6 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 0.3 kg glider and continues at 0.15 m s⁻¹.
(0.5)(0.6) = (0.5)(0.15) + (0.3)vB
vB = 0.225 ÷ 0.3 = 0.75 m s⁻¹.

Idea 3

Elastic and inelastic collisions

TypeMomentum conserved?K.E. conserved?Example
elastic✔✔ (total K.E. after = before)gliders with magnets / spring buffers; snooker balls (nearly)
inelastic✔✘ some K.E. lostmost real collisions: a ball bouncing, cars bumping
perfectly inelastic✔✘ maximum K.E. lossobjects stick together (Blu-Tack, car crash locking together)
✏️ Checking the typeFor the trolleys that stuck together (2 kg at 3 m s⁻¹ hits 1 kg at rest, v = 2 m s⁻¹):
K.E. before = ½(2)(3²) = 9 J; K.E. after = ½(3)(2²) = 6 J
3 J of K.E. is lost (to heat, sound, deformation) → perfectly inelastic.
Common mistakes ⚠️
  • “Momentum is lost in an inelastic collision.” ✘ Momentum is always conserved (no external force). Only K.E. is lost.
  • Total energy is still conserved: the lost K.E. becomes other forms.
  • In an explosion, the total K.E. increases (from chemical or elastic energy) but the total momentum stays the same (often zero).

Application

Mechanics in traffic accident investigation

✏️ Was the driver speeding?A 1200 kg car crashes into the back of a 1000 kg car waiting at a red light. The two cars lock together. From the skid marks, police find that the wreck moved off at 10 m s⁻¹ just after the impact.
  1. Conservation of momentum: (1200)u + (1000)(0) = (1200 + 1000)(10)
  2. u = 22 000 ÷ 1200 = 18.3 m s⁻¹ = 66 km/h → over a 50 km/h speed limit!
  3. K.E. before = ½(1200)(18.3²) ≈ 2.0 × 10⁵ J; after = ½(2200)(10²) = 1.1 × 10⁵ J → about 9 × 10⁴ J went into damage, heat and sound.
🚦 Why speeding is dangerous
  • Momentum ∝ v: a faster car needs a larger force (or longer time) to stop.
  • K.E. ∝ v²: at double the speed, there is 4 times as much energy to cause damage, and the braking distance is 4 times as long.

Here the conservation law gives the answer from the “before and after” only — Newton’s Second Law could not, because the crash forces are unknown.
