Lesson 2.6: Projectile Motion (拋體運動)

A basketball shot, a football kick, water from a hose: they all follow a curved path. We can work it out using two simple motions at the same time.

📌 Extension componentProjectile motion is underlined in the C&A Guide: it belongs to the extension part of the Compulsory Part.
Key wordShort meaning
projectile (拋體)An object thrown into the air that moves under gravity only.
trajectory (軌跡)The path of the projectile — a parabola (拋物線).
angle of projection (拋射角)The angle between the initial velocity and the horizontal.
time of flight (飛行時間), maximum height (最大高度), range (射程)Time in the air; highest point above the launch point; horizontal distance travelled.
You will needThe four equations of motion (Lesson 2.1b) and resolving vectors (Lesson 2.2b). Air resistance is ignored.

Idea 1

The path of a projectile

A multi-flash (strobe) photo (頻閃照片) or a slow-motion video of a ball thrown at an angle shows positions at equal time intervals:

  • Horizontally, the images are equally spaced → constant horizontal velocity (no horizontal force).
  • Vertically, the spacing changes just like a ball thrown straight up and falling back → acceleration g downward.
  • The path is a symmetrical curve called a parabola. The ball rises, reaches a highest point, and falls.
Projectile motion = two motions togetherhorizontal: uniform velocity (ax = 0)
vertical: uniform acceleration (ay = −g, up = +)
At the top, the vertical velocity is zero, but the ball still moves sideways: its speed is not zero, and its acceleration is still g downward.
🚌 Seen from a moving busThrow a ball straight up inside a bus moving at constant velocity: you see it go straight up and down. Someone on the pavement sees the same ball follow a parabola, because it also moves forward with the bus.
Idea 2

Horizontal and vertical motions are independent (互不影響)

🧪 Experiment

A spring device releases ball A (dropped from rest) and projects ball B horizontally at the same moment, from the same height.

  • You hear them hit the floor at the same time.
  • Flash photos show A and B at the same height at every instant.
Conclusion ⭐The horizontal velocity does not affect the vertical motion (and gravity does not change the horizontal velocity). We can treat the two directions separately. The only quantity they share is time t.
🐒 Monkey and hunterA hunter aims a dart directly at a monkey hanging in a tree. The monkey lets go at the instant the dart is fired. Both fall the same vertical distance ½gt² in the same time, so the dart still hits the monkey (if it reaches it before the ground). Gravity pulls both down equally.

Idea 3

Objects projected horizontally

Equations (up = +, launched horizontally with speed u)
Horizontal (x)Vertical (y)
ux = u, ax = 0uy = 0, ay = −g
vx = u (constant)vy = −gt
sx = utsy = −½gt²
✏️ A ball rolls off a table (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A ball leaves a 1.2 m high table horizontally at 3.0 m s⁻¹.
  1. Time to fall (vertical only): −1.2 = −½(9.81)t² → t = √(2.4 ÷ 9.81) = 0.495 s
  2. Horizontal distance: sx = ut = 3.0 × 0.495 = 1.48 m
  3. Velocity on landing: vx = 3.0, vy = −9.81 × 0.495 = −4.85 m s⁻¹
    speed = √(3.0² + 4.85²) = 5.70 m s⁻¹; angle below horizontal = tan⁻¹(4.85 ÷ 3.0) = 58.3°
Key pointThe time to fall depends only on the height, not on the horizontal speed. Rolling off faster → lands further away, but after the same time.
Idea 4

Objects projected at an angle θ

Resolve the initial velocityux = u cos θ   (stays constant)
uy = u sin θ   (changes: vy = u sin θ − gt)
sx = (u cos θ) t    sy = (u sin θ) t − ½gt²
  • Highest point: vy = 0 (vx is still u cos θ).
  • Same launch and landing level: sy = 0 → time of flight T = 2u sin θ ÷ g; range R = u cos θ × T = u² sin 2θ ÷ g → largest at θ = 45°. Angles θ and 90° − θ give the same range.
✏️ A football kicked at 20 m s⁻¹, 30° above the ground (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)
  1. ux = 20 cos 30° = 17.32 m s⁻¹; uy = 20 sin 30° = 10.0 m s⁻¹
  2. Time to top: 0 = 10 − 9.81t → t = 1.02 s
  3. Maximum height: 0 = 10² − 2(9.81)sy → sy = 5.10 m
  4. Time of flight (sy = 0): t = 2 × 1.02 = 2.04 s
  5. Range: sx = 17.32 × 2.04 = 35.3 m
