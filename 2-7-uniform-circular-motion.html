Lesson 2.7: Uniform Circular Motion (勻速圓周運動)

A Ferris wheel, a car on a roundabout, a satellite: each moves in a circle at constant speed. Yet its velocity keeps changing — so there must be a force!

📌 Extension componentUniform circular motion is underlined in the C&A Guide: it belongs to the extension part of the Compulsory Part.

period T (周期), frequency f (頻率)Time for one revolution; revolutions per second (f = 1/T, unit Hz).

Idea 1

Angular displacement, angular velocity and linear velocity

Radianθ (in rad) = arc length ÷ radius = s/r  ⇒  s = rθ
360° = 2π rad; 180° = π rad; 90° = π/2 rad
Angular velocity ⭐Angular velocity = rate of change of angular displacement
ω = Δθ ÷ Δt (rad s⁻¹)
One revolution: ω = 2π/T = 2πf
Relating to linear velocity ⭐In time Δt the object moves an arc Δs = rΔθ, so
v = rω
The velocity is along the tangent to the circle.
✏️ ExamplesMinute hand of a clock: T = 3600 s → ω = 2π ÷ 3600 = 1.75 × 10⁻³ rad s⁻¹.
A point on the equator: T = 1 day = 86 400 s → ω = 7.27 × 10⁻⁵ rad s⁻¹; with r = 6.37 × 10⁶ m, v = rω = 463 m s⁻¹.
On a merry-go-roundEveryone turns through the same angle in the same time: same ω. A child further out has a larger r, so a larger v (v = rω).

🎥 Activity: take a multi-flash photo or video of a puck whirled on an air table. Measure the angle turned between frames to find ω, and the arc between frames to check v = rω.

Idea 2

Centripetal acceleration

In uniform circular motion the speed is constant, but the direction of the velocity keeps changing. A change in velocity means there is an acceleration.

Centripetal acceleration ⭐a = v²/r = rω²
Direction: always towards the centre of the circle.
✏️ ExampleA car goes round a roundabout of radius 50 m at a steady 20 m s⁻¹.
a = v²/r = 20² ÷ 50 = 8 m s⁻² towards the centre.
v and a are always perpendicular.
Common mistake ⚠️“Uniform circular motion has constant velocity, so a = 0.” ✘ The speed is constant, but the velocity is not (its direction changes). a = v²/r ≠ 0.
Idea 3

The net force points towards the centre

Centripetal force ⭐By Newton’s Second Law, the acceleration towards the centre needs a net (resultant) force towards the centre:
F = ma = mv²/r = mrω²
  • Centripetal force is not a new type of force. It is the resultant of real forces: tension, friction, gravity, normal reaction…
  • It is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work: the speed (and K.E.) stays constant.
  • If the force disappears (string breaks), the object moves off along the tangent in a straight line (Newton’s First Law) — not outwards along the radius.
SituationCentripetal force provided by
stone whirled on a stringtension
car turning on a level roadfriction on the tyres
Moon / satellite orbiting Earthgravitational force
clothes in a spin dryer drumnormal reaction from the drum
🧪 Whirling a rubber bungA rubber bung is whirled in a horizontal circle on a string passing through a glass tube; the string holds a hanging load at the bottom. The tension (≈ weight of the load) provides the centripetal force. Time 20 revolutions to find T, then ω = 2π/T. Or attach the string to a force sensor: you find F ∝ ω² (fixed r) and F ∝ r (fixed ω), i.e. F = mrω².

Applications

Solving circular motion problems

Method ✍️1. Draw a free-body diagram. 2. Find the resultant force towards the centre. 3. Set it equal to mv²/r (or mrω²). 4. If needed, use “vertical forces balance”.
✏️ Car on a level roadA 1200 kg car turns on a level bend of radius 40 m. The maximum friction is 6000 N. Maximum speed:
friction = mv²/r → 6000 = 1200v²/40
v = √200 = 14.1 m s⁻¹. Faster → not enough friction → the car skids off along the tangent.
✏️ Flying chairs (conical pendulum) at Ocean Park, g = 10 m s⁻²A chair swings in a horizontal circle of radius 5.0 m with its chain at θ = 30° to the vertical.
Vertical: T cos θ = mg; horizontal: T sin θ = mv²/r
Divide: tan θ = v²/(rg) → v² = 5.0 × 10 × tan 30° = 28.9
v = 5.37 m s⁻¹ (independent of the rider’s mass!)
✏️ Ferris wheel at constant speed (g = 10 m s⁻²)A 50 kg rider, r = 10 m, v = 5 m s⁻¹. At the top, the net force is downward (towards the centre):
mg − R = mv²/r → R = 500 − 50 × 25 ÷ 10 = 375 N. She feels lighter at the top.
✈️ BankingA plane banks (tilts) to turn: the horizontal component of the lift provides the centripetal force. A road banked at θ with no friction works the same way: the horizontal component of the normal reaction gives mv²/r, so tan θ = v²/(rg).
