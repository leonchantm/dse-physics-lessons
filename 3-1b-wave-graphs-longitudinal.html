Lesson 3.1b: Particle Motion, Phase and Wave Graphs (質點運動、相位與波動圖像)

A wave is a pattern that moves, but each particle just goes up and down (or to and fro). In this lesson you learn to predict how each particle moves, compare particles using phase, and read the two kinds of wave graph — including the tricky graphs of longitudinal waves.

Key wordShort meaning
in phase (同相)Particles moving in step: same displacement, same direction, all the time.
antiphase (反相)Particles always moving in opposite directions, with opposite displacements.
displacement–distance graph (位移–距離關係線圖)A “photo” of all particles at one instant: shows A and λ.
displacement–time graph (位移–時間關係線圖)The motion of one particle over time: shows A and T.
compression / rarefaction (密部 / 疏部)Regions where particles are crowded / spread out.

Idea 1

Predicting the motion of a particle

The waveform moves along, so each particle takes the displacement that the particle behind it (nearer the source) had a moment ago.

The shift method ⭐
  1. Draw the wave again shifted a little in the direction of travel (dashed).
  2. A particle moves vertically from the solid curve to the dashed curve.
  • Particles at a crest or trough are momentarily at rest.
  • Particles at the equilibrium position move fastest.
  • Particles never move along the direction of travel of a transverse wave.
Common mistake ⚠️Particles do not slide along the curve. In a transverse wave they only move up or down. Reverse the direction of travel and every particle’s motion reverses.
Idea 2

Phase: comparing two particles

  • Particles one wavelength apart (or 2λ, 3λ …) are in phase: same displacement and same direction of motion at all times.
  • Particles half a wavelength apart (or 1.5λ, 2.5λ …) are in antiphase: always opposite.
  • Other separations give other phase differences.
⭐ Phase differencephase difference = (Δx / λ) × 360°
one complete cycle = 360°
✏️ Exampleλ = 1.2 m. P and Q are 0.3 m apart.
Δx/λ = 0.3/1.2 = ¼ ⇒ phase difference = 90°.
If they were 1.8 m apart: 1.8/1.2 = 1.5 ⇒ 540° ≡ 180°: antiphase.
Same for all particlesAll particles in a travelling wave vibrate with the same amplitude and the same frequency; they differ only in phase.

Sketching the s–t graph of particle P ⭐
  1. Read P’s displacement on the s–d graph at t = 0 (start of the s–t graph).
  2. Use the shift method to see whether P moves up or down next.
  3. Draw a sine curve of period T = 1/f with the same amplitude.
Read the axis label first ⚠️Many marks are lost by reading a period off an s–d graph or a wavelength off an s–t graph. Combine them with v = fλ = λ/T.

Idea 4

Longitudinal waves on a displacement graph

In a longitudinal wave the particles move along the direction of travel. We still draw a displacement graph, taking displacement to the right as positive.

Finding C and R on an s–d graph ⭐
  • Look at a point where s = 0. If particles on its left are displaced right (+) and particles on its right are displaced left (−), they crowd in: compression.
  • The opposite (left particles move left, right particles move right) gives a rarefaction.
  • Compression and the next rarefaction are λ/2 apart.
Examiners’ favourite trap ⚠️A particle at the centre of a compression (or rarefaction) is at its equilibrium position (s = 0) and is moving fastest. Particles with maximum displacement are midway between a compression and a rarefaction.
For a wave travelling to the right, a particle at a compression centre moves to the right (along the direction of travel); at a rarefaction centre it moves to the left.

Worked examples

DSE-style graph questions

✏️ Example 1: from an s–t graphThe s–t graph of a particle on a rope shows an amplitude of 3 cm and 5 complete cycles in 2.0 s. The wave speed is 1.5 m s⁻¹. Find f and λ.
T = 2.0 ÷ 5 = 0.40 s ⇒ f = 1/T = 2.5 Hz
λ = v/f = 1.5 ÷ 2.5 = 0.60 m
✏️ Example 2: two snapshotsA wave travels to the right. The waveform at t = 0.30 s (dashed) is the waveform at t = 0 shifted by λ/4 to the right. Find the possible periods if T > 0.2 s.
In 0.30 s the wave moved (n + ¼)λ, so 0.30 = (n + ¼)T, n = 0, 1, 2 …
n = 0: T = 1.2 s; n = 1: T = 0.30 ÷ 1.25 = 0.24 s; n = 2: T = 0.133 s (< 0.2 s, rejected).
Hidden trap ⚠️A wave looks the same after moving one whole wavelength, so a snapshot pair usually gives several possible answers. Read the question’s condition (e.g. “T > 0.2 s”, “shortest time”).
