Lesson 3.2: Reflection, Refraction and Diffraction of Waves (波的反射、折射和衍射)

All waves — water, sound and light — bounce off barriers, bend when their speed changes and spread round corners. We study these behaviours with water waves in a ripple tank, where we can actually see the wavefronts.

Key wordShort meaning
wavefront / ray (波陣面 / 射線)Line joining in-phase points (e.g. a crest) / arrow showing the direction of travel, ⊥ wavefront.
reflection (反射)Waves bounce off a barrier; angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
refraction (折射)Change of direction caused by a change of wave speed in a new medium.
refractive index (折射率)n = v₁/v₂: how much the wave slows down in medium 2.
diffraction (衍射)Waves spread out after passing through a gap or round an edge.

Idea 1

Watching water waves in a ripple tank

  • A shallow transparent tank of water is lit from above; the wave pattern is projected onto a screen or the floor.
  • A crest acts like a converging lens and gives a bright line; a trough gives a dark line.
  • A straight bar on the vibrator makes straight (plane) waves; a dipper makes circular waves.
  • Water surface waves are treated as transverse waves in this course.
Wavefronts and rays ⭐We draw crests as wavefronts. Neighbouring wavefronts are one wavelength apart. Rays (direction of travel) are perpendicular to the wavefronts.
🔬 Freezing the patternA stroboscope flashing at the vibrator frequency makes each crest move exactly one wavelength between flashes, so the pattern looks stationary and λ can be measured: measure across several wavefronts and divide. Then v = fλ.
Four wave phenomenaReflection, refraction, diffraction and interference. Particles can also bounce and change direction, but only waves show diffraction and interference — they are the “fingerprints” of waves.
Idea 2

Reflection of waves

⭐ Laws of reflection1. The angle of reflection = the angle of incidence (r = i).
2. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.
Drawing reflected wavefronts ⭐
  1. Draw an incident ray ⊥ the incident wavefronts.
  2. Draw the normal and the reflected ray with r = i.
  3. Draw the reflected wavefronts ⊥ the reflected ray, with the same spacing λ.
The wavefronts make the same angle with the barrier as the angle of incidence.

Circular waves: reflected circular waves look as if they come from the image of the dipper, the same distance behind the barrier as the dipper is in front.

Nothing else changes ⚠️Reflection happens in the same medium, so speed, frequency and wavelength are all unchanged. Only the direction changes.

Everyday examples: echoes (sound), radar (radio waves), water waves bouncing back from a sea wall.

Idea 3

Refraction: waves change speed

  • Water waves travel faster in deeper water.
  • The frequency stays the same (it is fixed by the source), so λ = v/f: deeper water → longer wavelength.
  • Deep → shallow: waves slow down, wavefronts get closer, the ray bends towards the normal.
  • Shallow → deep: they speed up and bend away from the normal.
  • If the waves arrive along the normal (θ₁ = 0) they slow down but do not change direction.
⭐ Refraction equation and refractive indexsin θ₁ / sin θ₂ = v₁ / v₂ = λ₁ / λ₂
Refractive index of medium 2 relative to medium 1: n = v₁ / v₂
(θ measured between the ray and the normal.)
Memory line ⭐larger v ⇔ longer λ ⇔ larger sin θ (always on the deep side).
Common mistake ⚠️“The frequency decreases in shallow water.” No: f is unchanged; it is v and λ that decrease together.
Idea 4

Diffraction: spreading through gaps and round corners

When waves pass through a gap or round the edge of an obstacle, they bend and spread into the region behind it. This is diffraction.

Degree of diffraction ⭐
  • Narrower gap → more spreading.
  • Longer wavelength → more spreading (lower f or deeper water).
  • Most obvious when the gap width is comparable to (or smaller than) λ; if a ≫ λ the waves pass almost straight through.
  • Amplitude does not affect the degree of diffraction.
What stays the same? ⚠️Diffraction happens in the same medium: λ, f and v are unchanged; only the shape of the wavefronts (and the direction of travel of parts of the wave) changes.
Everyday exampleYou can hear people talking round the corner of a doorway but not see them: sound wavelengths (about 0.02–17 m) are comparable to the doorway, light wavelengths (about 5 × 10⁻⁷ m) are far too small.
