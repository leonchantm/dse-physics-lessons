Lesson 3.3: Superposition and Interference (疊加和干涉)

When two waves meet they do not bounce off each other — they simply add up. With two steady sources this gives a fixed pattern of loud and quiet (or big and calm) regions called an interference pattern.

Key wordShort meaning
superposition (疊加)Resultant displacement = sum of the individual displacements.
coherent sources (相干波源)Same frequency and a constant phase difference.
constructive / destructive interference (相長 / 相消干涉)Waves arrive in phase (reinforce) / in antiphase (cancel).
path difference (程差)S₁P − S₂P: the difference in distances from the two sources.
antinodal / nodal line (波腹線 / 波節線)Line of constructive / destructive interference.

Idea 1

The principle of superposition

⭐ Principle of superposition1. When two waves meet, the resultant displacement is the (vector) sum of the individual displacements.
2. After they separate, each wave continues in its original direction with its original shape and amplitude.
  • Two upward pulses meeting give a momentarily bigger pulse.
  • An upward and a downward pulse partly or completely cancel. If they are identical the string is momentarily flat — but the particles are moving, so the energy is still there (as kinetic energy).
🔬 DemonstrationsSend pulses from both ends of a slinky spring. Or add two signals on a dual-trace CRO and display their sum.
Drawing the resultant ⭐At many points along the string, add the two displacements (with signs), plot the sums and join them smoothly.

Idea 2

Interference of waves from two coherent sources

Two dippers on the same vibrator make two sets of circular waves. Where they overlap they superpose:

  • Constructive interference: crest meets crest (or trough meets trough). Large amplitude.
  • Destructive interference: crest meets trough. Small amplitude (zero if the two amplitudes are equal: calm water).
Coherent sources ⭐A steady, observable interference pattern needs coherent sources: same frequency and a constant phase difference. Two dippers on one vibrator are coherent; two separate vibrators usually are not.
Common mistake ⚠️On an antinodal line the water is not always at a crest: it oscillates with a large amplitude (crest, then trough …). On a nodal line it stays (almost) still.
Idea 3

Conditions in terms of path difference

Path difference at P = S₁P − S₂P (take the size). Each extra wavelength of path delays one wave by one whole cycle.

SourcesConstructive interferenceDestructive interference
in phasepath difference = nλ (0, λ, 2λ …)path difference = (n + ½)λ (½λ, 1½λ …)
antiphasepath difference = (n + ½)λpath difference = nλ
✏️ ExampleIn-phase sources, λ = 2.0 cm. S₁P = 15 cm, S₂P = 10 cm.
Path difference = 5.0 cm = 2.5λ ⇒ destructive.
If the sources were in antiphase ⇒ constructive.
Method ⭐1. Find the path difference. 2. Divide by λ. 3. Whole number or half-number? 4. Check whether the sources are in phase or in antiphase.

Idea 4

Nodal lines, antinodal lines and what affects them

  • Joining points with the same path difference gives a curved line (a hyperbola) between and beyond the sources.
  • Antinodal lines: constructive (path difference 0, λ, 2λ … for in-phase sources). The central line (perpendicular bisector of S₁S₂) has path difference 0.
  • Nodal lines: destructive (½λ, 1½λ …). Antinodal and nodal lines alternate.
Factors ⭐The lines spread farther apart when
  • the wavelength is longer (lower frequency), or
  • the source separation d is smaller.
Counting antinodal lines (in-phase sources)Path difference can never exceed d, so nλ ≤ d.
If n is the largest whole number with n ≤ d/λ, there are 2n + 1 antinodal lines (n on each side plus the central line).
e.g. d = 10 cm, λ = 3 cm: n = 3 ⇒ 7 antinodal lines.
Antiphase sources ⚠️Everything swaps: the central line becomes a nodal line, and antinodal lines are where the path difference is ½λ, 1½λ …
Worked example

A ripple-tank interference problem

✏️ Two dippersTwo dippers S₁ and S₂ vibrate at 4.0 Hz and make waves of wavelength 5.0 cm. P is a point with S₁P = 22.5 cm and S₂P = 10.0 cm. Q is the midpoint of S₁S₂.
  1. Path difference at P = 22.5 − 10.0 = 12.5 cm = 2.5λ; at Q it is 0.
  2. Dippers in phase: P destructive (nodal), Q constructive. Dippers in antiphase: P constructive, Q destructive.
  3. Lines from Q to P inclusive (in phase): antinodal at 0, λ, 2λ (3 lines) and nodal at 0.5λ, 1.5λ, 2.5λ (3 lines): 6 lines.
  4. The frequency is doubled (in phase): λ = 2.5 cm, path difference at P = 12.5 cm = 5λ ⇒ P becomes constructive.
Exam tip ⚠️Always state the path difference in terms of λ and say whether the sources are in phase before concluding. For 2 marks: path difference = 2.5λ (1M), so destructive interference (1A).
