Lesson 3.4: Stationary Waves (駐波)

Pluck a guitar string or shake a rope tied to a wall at just the right frequency and the wave seems to stand still: some points never move while others swing wildly. This is a stationary (standing) wave.

Key wordShort meaning
stationary wave (駐波)Pattern formed by two identical waves travelling in opposite directions; the waveform does not move along.
node N (波節)Point that is always at rest (zero amplitude).
antinode A (波腹)Point with the largest amplitude.
loop (波環)Section between two neighbouring nodes; length λ/2.

Idea 1

How a stationary wave forms

  • A stationary wave forms when two identical waves (same frequency, same amplitude, same speed) travel along the same line in opposite directions and superpose.
  • On a string it is usually the incident wave and its reflection from the fixed end.
  • A wave reflected at a fixed end is inverted (180° phase change), so the fixed end is always a node.
🔬 ExperimentA vibrator drives one end of a string; the other end is fixed (e.g. over a pulley with a hanging mass). Adjust the vibrator frequency until clear loops appear. The loops look solid because of persistence of vision.
⭐ Condition on a string of length LThe string length must fit a whole number of loops:
L = nλ/2  (n = 1, 2, 3 …), so λ = 2L/n
Since v is fixed by the string, the frequencies are f = v/λ = nv/(2L).
Idea 2

Characteristics of a stationary wave

  • Nodes are always at rest; antinodes have the largest amplitude. Other points have amplitudes in between: the amplitude varies along the wave.
  • Neighbouring nodes (or antinodes) are λ/2 apart; a node and the next antinode are λ/4 apart.
  • All particles vibrate with the same frequency.
  • Particles in the same loop are in phase; particles in neighbouring loops are in antiphase.
  • The waveform does not move along and no energy is transferred along the string (energy is confined).
At an instant of maximum displacement ⭐All particles are momentarily at rest together, and all of them reach the equilibrium position at the same moment (T/4 later). Above: P and S (loops 1 and 3) are in phase; R (loop 2) is in antiphase with both.

Idea 3

Comparing stationary and travelling waves

Stationary waveTravelling wave
waveformdoes not move alongmoves forward at speed v
vibrationparticles at nodes do not vibrateall particles vibrate
frequencyall particles have the same frequency
amplitudevaries: 0 at nodes, maximum at antinodesthe same for all particles
phasein phase within a loop; antiphase in neighbouring loopsphase changes continuously along the wave
energyconfined, not transferredtransferred along the wave
Common mistake ⚠️“Increasing the frequency makes the waves on the string faster, so there are more loops.” No: v is fixed by the string (tension and mass per unit length). Higher f ⇒ shorter λ ⇒ more loops.

Worked example

Stationary wave on a string

✏️ ExampleA string 1.5 m long, fixed at one end, shows 5 loops when the vibrator frequency is 50 Hz.
(a) λ = 2L/n = 2 × 1.5 ÷ 5 = 0.60 m
(b) v = fλ = 50 × 0.60 = 30 m s⁻¹
(c) For 3 loops: v is unchanged, λ = 2 × 1.5 ÷ 3 = 1.0 m, f = 30 ÷ 1.0 = 30 Hz
(Shortcut: f ∝ number of loops, so f = 50 × 3/5 = 30 Hz.)
Exam tip ⚠️
  • Count loops, not nodes: n loops have n + 1 nodes.
  • Neighbouring nodes are λ/2 apart, so λ = 2 × (node spacing).
  • Keep v the same unless the tension or the string changes.

Guide: “stationary wave (transverse waves only)” — this is core, not extension; harmonics/overtones of instruments are not required (notes Chapter 9 marks them out of syllabus). Exam emphasis: nodes and the number of loops, λ = 2L/n, frequency for a given number of loops with v unchanged (Paper 1B 2025 Q10 was poorly answered), phase of particles in different loops, “all particles momentarily at rest at maximum displacement”. Simulation model: string L = 1.2 m with v = 24 m s⁻¹ (independent of f), so resonances occur at f = 10n Hz (matches the notes’ example: 60 Hz gives 6 loops, λ = 0.4 m). Steady-state shape y = A(f) sin k(L − x) cos ωt with A(f) ∝ 1/√(sin²kL + δ²) (small damping δ); its two travelling components are ½A sin(ωt − kx + kL) and −½A sin(ωt + kx − kL).
