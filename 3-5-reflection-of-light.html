Lesson 3.5: Reflection of Light and Plane Mirrors (光的反射與平面鏡)

You see this page because light from it enters your eyes. In this lesson you learn how light reflects, why your image in a mirror seems to be behind the glass, and how to draw ray diagrams for mirrors.

Key wordShort meaning
luminous object (發光體)Gives out its own light (Sun, bulb, candle flame).
normal (法線)Line perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray hits it.
angle of incidence / reflection (入射角 / 反射角)Angles between the normal and the incident / reflected ray.
real / virtual image (實像 / 虛像)Light really passes through it (can be caught on a screen) / light only seems to come from it.
laterally inverted (左右倒轉)Left and right are swapped.

Idea 1

Light rays and the laws of reflection

  • Luminous objects give out light; non-luminous objects (the Moon, a book, people) are seen because they reflect light into our eyes.
  • Light travels in straight lines. A ray is a straight line with an arrow; a beam can be parallel, diverging or converging.
⭐ Laws of reflection1. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection: i = r.
2. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.
Angles are measured from the normal, not from the mirror.
Regular vs diffuse reflectionRegular: a smooth flat surface (mirror, calm water) reflects a parallel beam as a parallel beam ⇒ clear image.
Diffuse: a rough surface (paper, wall, blackboard) scatters the light in many directions ⇒ no image, but the surface can be seen from everywhere.
Every single ray still obeys the laws of reflection in both cases.
Common mistake ⚠️If a ray makes 35° with the mirror, the angle of incidence is 90° − 35° = 55°.
Idea 2

The image formed by a plane mirror

Rays from the object reflect off the mirror into the eye. The brain assumes light travels in straight lines, so the light seems to come from a point behind the mirror: the image. No light really goes there, so it is a virtual image (drawn dashed).

Nature of the image ⭐
  • virtual (cannot be caught on a screen)
  • erect (upright)
  • same size as the object
  • laterally inverted
  • as far behind the mirror as the object is in front: v = u, on the normal through the object
Drawing a ray diagram ⭐1. Locate the image I (same distance behind, on the normal).
2. Draw dashed lines from I to the mirror, aimed at the eye.
3. Draw solid rays from those points on the mirror to the eye, with arrows.
4. Draw solid rays from the object O to the same points on the mirror.

Idea 3

Mirror applications and favourite exam set-ups

Full-length mirror ⭐To see your whole body you need a mirror at least half your height, with its bottom at half your eye height. This does not depend on how far you stand from the mirror (similar triangles).
Rotating a mirrorIf the incident ray is fixed and the mirror turns through θ, the reflected ray turns through 2θ.
Moving mirror or moving personYou walk towards a fixed mirror at v: your image moves towards the mirror at v, so it approaches you at 2v. If the mirror moves away at v, your image moves away from you (and from the ground) at 2v.
🔭 PeriscopeTwo parallel plane mirrors at 45° to the tube. Light reflects twice. Final image: virtual, erect, same size, not laterally inverted (two lateral inversions cancel), and farther away by the length of the tube.
Two mirrors at an angle θImages of images form. Number of images n = 360°/θ − 1 (when 360°/θ is a whole even number): 90° → 3, 60° → 5. Parallel mirrors (θ → 0, e.g. in a lift) give a very large number of images.
Common mistake ⚠️A clock seen in a mirror reading 3:00 actually shows 9:00 (lateral inversion).
Worked examples

Plane-mirror calculations

✏️ Example 1: smallest full-length mirrorMandy is 1.70 m tall and her eyes are 1.60 m above the floor. She stands 0.8 m from a wall mirror.
Ray from the image of her feet to her eye meets the mirror halfway up: bottom of mirror = 1.60 ÷ 2 = 0.80 m above the floor.
Ray from the image of the top of her head: top of mirror = (1.70 + 1.60) ÷ 2 = 1.65 m.
Minimum length = 1.65 − 0.80 = 0.85 m = half her height. Her distance (0.8 m) does not matter.
✏️ Example 2: moving imagesKen stands 3.0 m in front of a mirror.
(a) Distance to his image = 3.0 + 3.0 = 6.0 m.
(b) He walks towards the mirror at 1.5 m s⁻¹. His image moves towards the mirror at 1.5 m s⁻¹, so it approaches Ken at 3.0 m s⁻¹.
(c) Instead, the mirror moves away from Ken at 0.5 m s⁻¹: his image moves away at 1.0 m s⁻¹ (relative to the ground).

Exam emphasis: ray diagrams must start from the image: draw dashed lines from the image to the mirror and solid rays from the mirror to the eye, then solid rays from the object to the same points on the mirror, with arrows. Simulation model: mirror on the line x = 0; image of a point (x, y) at (−x, y); the ray reaching the eye meets the mirror where the image–eye line crosses x = 0, and is visible only if that point lies on the mirror. Two-mirror images are the orbit of the object under reflections in the two mirror lines (dihedral group), giving 360°/θ − 1 images when 360°/θ is an even integer.
