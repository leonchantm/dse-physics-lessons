Lesson 3.6b: Total Internal Reflection (全內反射)

Why do diamonds sparkle, and how can light travel round corners inside a thin glass fibre carrying your internet data? The answer is total internal reflection: under the right conditions a boundary reflects all the light, better than any mirror.

Key wordShort meaning
total internal reflection (TIR) (全內反射)All light is reflected back into the denser medium.
critical angle C (臨界角)Angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90°.
optical fibre (光纖)Thin glass core with cladding that guides light by TIR.

Idea 1

When does total internal reflection happen?

Send light from glass into air. As the angle of incidence i increases, the refracted ray bends further from the normal and gets dimmer, while the reflected ray gets brighter.

  • At i = C (the critical angle) the refracted ray just grazes the surface (r = 90°).
  • For i > C there is no refracted ray: all the light is reflected.
⭐ Two conditions for TIR1. Light travels from an optically denser medium towards an optically less dense one (larger n → smaller n).
2. The angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle.
Common mistake ⚠️TIR can never happen for light going from air into glass or water, however large the angle.
Idea 2

Calculating the critical angle

⭐ Critical angleAt i = C, r = 90°. Snell’s law gives n sin C = 1 × sin 90°, so
sin C = 1/n  (medium to air)
Between two media (n₁ > n₂): n₁ sin C = n₂, i.e. sin C = n₂/n₁
boundaryC
water–air (n = 1.33)48.8°
glass–air (n = 1.50)41.8°
diamond–air (n = 2.42)24.4°
glass–watersin C = 1.33/1.50 ⇒ 62.5°
Method ⭐1. Find C for the boundary. 2. Find the angle of incidence (geometry of the block!). 3. Compare: i > C ⇒ TIR; i < C ⇒ refraction (with some reflection).
Larger n ⇒ smaller CDiamond’s very small critical angle (24.4°) means light entering a well-cut diamond is totally reflected many times before leaving through the top: it sparkles.

Idea 3

Total internal reflection at work

📶 Optical fibresA glass core surrounded by a cladding of lower refractive index. Light hits the core–cladding boundary at angles greater than C and is totally reflected again and again, even round gentle bends. Uses: telecommunication (more data, less loss, thinner and lighter than copper cables, no electrical interference) and endoscopes in medicine. The cladding protects the core surface (scratches, oil or touching fibres would let light leak).
🔺 45° prismsGlass has C ≈ 42° < 45°, so light hitting the long face of a 45°–90°–45° prism at 45° is totally reflected. Prisms replace mirrors in periscopes, binoculars and bicycle/car reflectors: brighter images, no multiple images from a silvered back, no tarnishing.
🐟 Looking up from under waterA diver sees the whole sky squeezed into a bright circle (rays from the horizon enter at about 48.8° to the vertical). Outside this circle the surface acts as a mirror reflecting the pool bottom by TIR. Radius of the circle = depth × tan C.
🏜️ MiragesAir near hot ground is hotter and less dense (smaller n). Light from the sky bends gradually away from the normal and is finally totally reflected upwards: the road looks wet (an image of the sky).
Any wave can do itTIR happens for sound and ultrasound too, when the wave goes into a medium in which it travels faster at more than the critical angle.
Worked examples

TIR calculations

✏️ Example 1: an optical fibreCore n₁ = 1.55, cladding n₂ = 1.45.
(a) sin C = 1.45/1.55 = 0.935 ⇒ C = 69.3°
(b) Inside the core, the ray must meet the wall at more than 69.3°, i.e. make less than 90° − 69.3° = 20.7° with the axis.
(c) At the end face (air → core): sin θ = 1.55 sin 20.7° = 0.548 ⇒ largest entry angle θ = 33.2°.
✏️ Example 2: a diver’s windowA diver is 1.5 m below the surface (n = 1.33) looking up.
C = sin⁻¹(1/1.33) = 48.8°
Radius of the bright circle = 1.5 × tan 48.8° = 1.71 m.
Geometry trap ⚠️The angle of incidence is measured from the normal to the surface where the ray hits, not from the axis of the fibre or from the surface of the prism.
