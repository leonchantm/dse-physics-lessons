Lesson 3.7b: The Lens Formula (透鏡公式) Extension

Ray diagrams show you roughly where an image is. The lens formula tells you exactly. You will also find the focal length from a straight-line graph, a favourite DSE experiment question.

Idea 1 Extension

The lens formula

Lens formula (real is positive)1/u + 1/v = 1/f
Real distances (real object, real image, real focus of a convex lens) are positive; virtual ones are negative.
Magnification (core)m = hᵢ / hₒ = |v| / u
Reading your answer ⭐
  • v > 0 → real, inverted, on the other side of the lens.
  • v < 0 → virtual, erect, on the same side as the object.
  • For a concave lens, put f in as a negative number.
Common mistakes ⚠️Forgetting to take the reciprocal at the end (1/v = 0.05 cm⁻¹ means v = 20 cm, not 0.05 cm); using + for a concave lens; mixing cm and m (just keep all distances in the same unit).
Worked examples Extension

Worked examples

Example 1: convex lens, real imagef = 12 cm, u = 20 cm.
1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/12 − 1/20 = 1/30 (1M)
v = 30 cm (+ ⇒ real, other side) (1A)
m = 30/20 = 1.5: real, inverted, magnified.
Example 2: convex lens, virtual imagef = 20 cm, u = 12 cm.
1/v = 1/20 − 1/12 = −1/30
v = −30 cm (− ⇒ virtual, same side as the object)
m = 30/12 = 2.5: virtual, erect, magnified (a magnifying glass).
Example 3: concave lensf = −10 cm, u = 15 cm.
1/v = −1/10 − 1/15 = −1/6
v = −6 cm: virtual; m = 6/15 = 0.4 (erect, diminished).
Example 4: finding fA sharp real image is on a screen 15 cm from a lens when the object is 30 cm away.
1/f = 1/30 + 1/15 = 3/30 ⇒ f = +10 cm (convex).

Idea 2 Extension

Combining the formula with magnification

Fixed object–screen distanceAn object and a screen are 100 cm apart. A convex lens (f = 20 cm) between them forms a sharp magnified image. Find u.
u + v = 100 and 1/u + 1/(100 − u) = 1/20
⇒ u² − 100u + 2000 = 0 ⇒ u = 27.6 cm or 72.4 cm.
Magnified ⇒ u < v: u = 27.6 cm, v = 72.4 cm, m = 2.62.
(The other position gives a diminished image, m = 0.382.)
Given the magnificationA concave lens (f = −12 cm) forms an image one third the size of the object. Find u.
Virtual: v = −u/3 ⇒ 1/u − 3/u = −1/12 ⇒ −2/u = −1/12
u = 24 cm, v = −8 cm.
Strategy ⭐Write m as a relation between v and u (real image: v = mu; virtual image: v = −mu), substitute into the lens formula, then solve.

Idea 3 Extension

Finding f from a graph

Linearise the lens formula1/v = −(1/u) + 1/f
Compare with y = mx + c: plot 1/v (y) against 1/u (x).
• straight line, slope −1
• y-intercept = 1/f and x-intercept = 1/f
Reading the graph ⭐If the y-intercept is 0.050 cm⁻¹, f = 1/0.050 = 20 cm. A lens with a longer focal length gives a line with smaller intercepts (parallel line, closer to the origin).
v against u?A graph of v against u is a curve (not linear), approaching v = f for large u; it crosses the line v = u at u = v = 2f, which is another way to find f.
Experiment Extension

Experiment: measuring f with the lens formula

Procedure (DSE style: imperative steps)
  1. Set up an illuminated object (cross-wire), the convex lens and a screen on a metre rule, all at the same height.
  2. Place the object at a distance u (greater than f) from the lens.
  3. Move the screen until a sharp image is formed; measure v.
  4. Repeat for several different values of u.
  5. Plot 1/v against 1/u; draw the best-fit straight line.
  6. f = 1/(y-intercept) (or 1/(x-intercept)).
Accuracy tips
  • Find the sharp image by moving the screen back and forth and taking the middle position.
  • The object must be beyond F′, otherwise no real image can be caught on the screen.
  • Do a rough check first with a distant object (image ≈ at F).
  • Using the graph averages out random errors in u and v.
