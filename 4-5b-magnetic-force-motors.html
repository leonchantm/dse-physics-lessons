Lesson 4.5b: Magnetic Force and Motors （磁力與電動機）

A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force. The size is F = B I ℓ sinθ. That force on opposite sides of a coil makes a turning couple — and with a split-ring commutator, you have a simple d.c. motor.

Hong Kong hook: the motor in a desk fan, a hairdryer, an MTR escalator drive, or an electric minibus wheel all use the same idea: force on currents in a magnetic field. School kits with a coil, magnets, and carbon brushes are the same physics in miniature.

This lesson (core) Force on a current-carrying conductor; F = BIl sinθ; turning effect on a coil; structure and operation of a simple d.c. motor; related problems. The force on a single moving charge (F = BQv sinθ) is Extension — that is Lesson 4.5c.

Learn Idea 1

Force on a current-carrying wire （載流導線所受磁力）

A current-carrying wire makes its own field (Lesson 4.5a). In an external B-field it feels a magnetic force. Classic demo: a free copper rod on rails between slab magnets rolls when the current is switched on.

B from N to S (right). I into the paper (×). Fleming’s left hand gives F downward.

Relative directions (Fleming’s left-hand rule)
  • First finger → magnetic field B
  • seCond finger → conventional current I
  • thuMb → magnetic force F
Hold the three fingers of your left hand at right angles. Align First with B and seCond with I; the thumb then shows F. Reverse B or reverse I → F reverses. If I is parallel to B, F is zero (see next step).
Remember Electric force on a charge is parallel to E. Magnetic force on a current is perpendicular to both B and I — never invent a force along the wire or along B.
Symbols into / out of paper × = into the paper (arrow tail). · (dot) = out of the paper (arrow tip). Useful when B or I is drawn on the page face-on.

Chinese: magnetic force 磁力, current-carrying conductor 載流導體, Fleming’s left-hand rule 弗萊明左手定則. Tip: left hand for motors; right hand later for generators.

Learn Idea 2

F = BIl sinθ and turning effect on a coil （公式與線圈轉矩）

A current balance shows F grows with B, I, and the length ℓ of wire inside the field. The angle between I and B also matters.

Force on a straight wire F = B I ℓ sinθ
θ = angle between the directions of I and B. Units: B in T, I in A, ℓ in m, F in N.
Definition link: 1 T is the field that gives 1 N on a 1 m wire carrying 1 A when θ = 90°.
  • θ = 90° (I ⊥ B) → sinθ = 1 → Fmax = BIl
  • θ = 0° or 180° (I ∥ B) → sinθ = 0 → F = 0
  • θ = 30° → sinθ = ½ → F = ½ BIl
Factors (keep these clear) F increases if you use a stronger magnet (↑B), a larger current (↑I), a longer active length in the field (↑ℓ), or an angle closer to 90°. Doubling B doubles F; halving θ from 90° to 30° does not halve F — use sinθ.

Turning effect on a coil

Place a rectangular coil PQRT in a uniform B with the plane of the coil parallel to B. Current on side PQ is opposite to current on side RT. Fleming’s left hand gives equal forces on PQ and RT in opposite directions — a couple. That couple produces a turning moment (torque) about the coil’s axis.

  • Sides parallel to B feel no force (I ∥ B).
  • As the coil turns, the moment arm changes. When the coil’s normal is parallel to B, the turning moment is zero — inertia can carry it past.
  • Without reversing I, the couple later reverses sense — the coil only rocks. Continuous rotation needs a commutator (next step).
What strengthens the turning effect? More turns N, stronger B, larger coil area in the field, larger I, and inserting a soft-iron core (concentrates flux). Practical motors also use curved magnets for a radial field so the torque stays healthier through the turn.

Chinese: turning effect / torque 轉矩／力矩, couple 力偶, current balance 電流天平.

Learn Idea 3

Simple d.c. motor （直流電動機）

A motor converts electrical → kinetic energy using the coil’s turning effect. A simple motor has magnets, a coil, a split-ring commutator, and two carbon brushes to a d.c. supply.

Snapshot: forces on opposite sides form a couple. The split ring and brushes reverse I every half-turn.

Role of the split-ring commutator
  • The commutator is two half-rings fixed to the coil; it rotates with the coil and rubs against the brushes.
  • Every half-turn, each half-ring swaps which brush it touches → the current in the coil reverses.
  • Reversing I reverses the force directions on the sides → the couple keeps driving the coil in the same sense (e.g. always the same sense (clockwise in the diagram)).
  • When the coil is vertical, the brushes may briefly leave a gap (no contact) → no current for an instant; the coil continues because of inertia.
Why not just leave I fixed? Without reversing I, after half a turn the couple would reverse sense and the coil would swing back. The split ring is the trick that makes continuous rotation with a d.c. supply.
Brushes Carbon brushes slide on the commutator and wear out over time. Brushless motors reverse currents electronically instead — same physics of F on currents, different switching method.
Energy picture (brief) Electrical power in: Pin = VI. Useful mechanical power out: force × speed (or torque × angular speed). Losses: heating in the coil (I²R) and friction. Efficiency = Pout / Pin.

Chinese: d.c. motor 直流電動機, commutator / split ring 整流子／半環, brush 電刷. Generators (4.6b) look similar but use the right-hand rule.

Learn Worked examples

Worked examples

Example 1 — F at 90°

B = 0.50 T, I = 3.0 A, ℓ = 0.20 m, θ = 90°. Find F.

1 Formula: F = B I ℓ sinθ.
2 sin 90° = 1 → F = BIl.
3 F = 0.50 × 3.0 × 0.20 = 0.30 N.
4 Direction: use Fleming’s left hand once B and I directions are given on a diagram.
Example 2 — angle not 90°

Same B, I, ℓ as Example 1, but θ = 30°. Find F.

1 F = BIl sinθ.
2 sin 30° = 0.50.
3 F = 0.50 × 3.0 × 0.20 × 0.50 = 0.15 N.
4 Check: half of the 90° result — because sin 30° = ½, not because θ itself is half.
Example 3 — Earth’s field on a vertical wire

A vertical wire ℓ = 0.50 m carries I = 8.0 A. Earth’s B dips at 20° to the horizontal. The magnetic force is 7.5 × 10−5 N. Find B.

1 Vertical I; B dips 20° to horizontal → angle between I and B is θ = 70°.
2 F = BIl sinθ ⇒ B = F / (I ℓ sinθ).
3 B = 7.5×10−5 / (8.0 × 0.50 × sin 70°).
4 sin 70° ≈ 0.940 → B ≈ 2.0 × 10−5 T (Earth’s order).
Example 4 — why the motor keeps turning

In a simple d.c. motor, the coil reaches the vertical position where the magnetic couple is briefly zero. Why does the coil not stop?

  1. At that instant brushes may lose contact → I = 0 → no magnetic torque.
  2. The coil already has angular speed → inertia carries it past the vertical.
  3. After the half-turn, the split ring has reversed I → the couple again drives in the same rotational sense.
  4. So continuous rotation needs both the commutator (correct sense of couple) and inertia (through the dead point).
Method habit (1) Write F = BIl sinθ. (2) Check θ — is I really perpendicular to B? (3) Substitute with units in SI. (4) For direction, draw B and I, then apply Fleming’s left hand. For motors, explain split ring + inertia separately.

Learn Summary

Remember

  • F = B I ℓ sinθ — force on a straight current-carrying wire; max when I ⊥ B.
  • Fleming’s left hand: First = B, seCond = I, thuMb = F.
  • A coil in uniform B feels a couple → turning effect; soft-iron core / more turns / larger B or I strengthen it.
  • Simple d.c. motor: magnets + coil + split-ring commutator + brushes; commutator reverses I each half-turn; inertia carries past the dead point.
  • Single-charge formula F = BQv is Lesson 4.5c (Extension).
