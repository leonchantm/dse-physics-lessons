Lesson 5.2a: Half-life, Activity and Safety （半衰期、活度與安全）

Half-life is the time for half the undecayed nuclei (or the activity) to fall to half. Activity counts decays per second. You will also correct for background and practise school-lab safety.

Hong Kong hook: granite and building materials add a little radon to indoor air; airline crews see higher cosmic-ray background — both reason we teach careful measurement and “time, distance, shielding.”

In this lesson Definition of t½; N = N₀ (½)n and A = A₀ (½)n; activity in Bq; reading t½ from a decay curve; background correction; dose in Sv and lab safety. Continuous exponential law and decay constant k → Lesson 5.2b (pointer only).

Learn Idea 1

Half-life （半衰期）

Radioactive decay is random for a single nucleus — you cannot say exactly when one nucleus will decay. But for a large sample, the number of undecayed nuclei falls to half after a fixed time.

Definition ⭐ t½ (half-life) = time for the number of radioactive nuclei (or the activity) to decrease by a factor of one-half.
  • t½ is a characteristic of the radionuclide.
  • It does not depend on how much sample you have, or on ordinary environmental factors (temperature, pressure) in school-level treatment.
  • Examples span seconds to billions of years (e.g. Na-24 ≈ 15 h; C-14 ≈ 5730 y; U-235 ≈ 7×108 y).
Random but predictable as a group Count rates wobble about a mean (random). The half-life still lets you predict how the average N or A falls over many nuclei.
Must remember t½: time for N (or A) → half. Characteristic of the nuclide; independent of amount / ordinary environment.
Learn Idea 2

Counting half-lives （半衰期次數）

After n half-lives, the remaining fraction is (½)n.

Formula ⭐ N = N₀ (½)n   where n = t / t½ (number of half-lives elapsed)

Sodium-24 (notes) t½ ≈ 15 h. Start with 40 billion undecayed nuclei → after 15 h: 20 billion; after another 15 h: 10 billion (one-quarter left). Same pattern every 15 h.
Must remember N = N₀ (½)n. Each half-life halves what is left — not the original amount again from scratch in a confusing way: always half of current remaining.
Learn Idea 3

Activity （活度）

Activity A measures how strong a sample is: the number of nuclei decaying per unit time.

Unit 1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second (becquerel)
School sealed sources are typically of order ~105 Bq (notes) — small, but still handled with rules.
  • More undecayed nuclei → higher activity: A ∝ N.
  • So activity follows the same half-life pattern:
Activity after n half-lives A = A₀ (½)n
What activity depends on Depends on how many active nuclei remain and on the nuclide’s half-life (faster half-life → higher activity for the same N). It does not depend on whether the radiation is α, β or γ for the definition of A itself.
Must remember A in Bq; A ∝ N; A = A₀ (½)n.
Learn Idea 4

Decay curve （衰變曲線）

A graph of N vs t (or A vs t) is a decay curve. Because A ∝ N, both have the same shape.

Find t½: pick a value on the vertical axis, find when it has halved, read Δt. Check at more than one place on the curve.

How to read t½
  1. Note A₀ (or N₀) at t = 0.
  2. Find the time when the reading is ½ A₀ — that time is t½.
  3. Optional check: time from ½ A₀ to ¼ A₀ should be another t½.
Must remember Decay curve: N–t or A–t. t½ = time for the plotted quantity to half.
Learn Idea 5

Background radiation （本底輻射）

A GM counter never reads exactly zero with no school source nearby. Background comes from rocks / building materials, radon, cosmic rays, and traces in the environment.

Corrected count rate corrected = measured − background
Use the corrected rate when finding half-life from count-rate data.
Graph that flattens

Measured count rate falls then levels off (~40 cpm in a notes example). The flat part ≈ background. Initial corrected rate = measured − background; find when corrected rate halves.

1 Read background from the late-time plateau.
2 Correct every useful point: measured − background.
3 Find time for corrected rate to fall to half → t½.
HK / STSE Higher altitude → more cosmic rays (airline crews). Indoor radon can rise in poorly ventilated spaces — one reason buildings and granite geology appear in safety talk.
Must remember Always subtract background before analysing a source’s count rate.
Learn Idea 6

Radiation safety （輻射安全）

Ionising radiation can damage cells. Equivalent dose is measured in sievert (Sv) (accounts for biological effect).

Reduce dose: time · distance · shielding
  • Time: shorter exposure → smaller dose.
  • Distance: stand further away; intensity falls rapidly with distance for a point-like source.
  • Shielding: appropriate absorber (link to 5.1a: paper / Al / lead depending on type).
School lab rules (age-appropriate)
  • Use tongs / handling tools; never touch the active face of a sealed source.
  • Keep sources in labelled containers; return to storage promptly.
  • Do not eat or drink near sources; wash hands after the practical.
  • Point sources away from people; use the shielding provided.
Attitude Respect sources without panic. School activities use weak sealed sources under teacher supervision — follow instructions exactly.
Must remember Dose unit Sv. Minimise with less time, more distance, shielding. Lab: tongs, store, no food, follow teacher.
Learn Worked examples

Worked examples

Count how many times the quantity halves (n), then use t = n × t½ or N = N₀ (½)n.

Example 1 — remaining nuclei

N₀ = 800. How many remain after 3 half-lives?

1 After 1: 400; after 2: 200; after 3: 100.
2 Or N = 800 × (½)3 = 800 × ⅛ = 100.
Example 2 — time from activity

Activity falls from 800 Bq to 100 Bq. t½ = 2 h. Find the time taken.

1 800 → 400 → 200 → 100 = 3 half-lives (fraction ⅛).
2 t = 3 × 2 h = 6 h.
Example 3 — find t½ from A data

A₀ = 2000 Bq; after 4 h, A = 125 Bq. Find t½.

1 2000 → 1000 → 500 → 250 → 125 = 4 halvings.
2 4 half-lives in 4 h → t½ = 1 h (60 min).
