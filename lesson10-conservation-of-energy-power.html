Lesson 10: Conservation of Energy & Power (能量守恆和功率)

On a roller coaster at Ocean Park, P.E. turns into K.E. and back again. Where does the energy go, and how fast can a machine transfer it?

Key wordShort meaning
law of conservation of energy (能量守恆定律)Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
mechanical energy (機械能)P.E. + K.E.
energy loss (能量損失)Mechanical energy changed to internal energy (heat) and sound, e.g. by friction.

Idea 1

The law of conservation of energy

Law of conservation of energy ⭐Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be converted from one form to another (or transferred). The total energy of a closed system stays constant.

A closed system is one where no energy enters or leaves. Examples of energy changes:

  • 💡 Lamp: electrical → light + internal energy
  • 🏃 Runner: chemical → K.E. + internal energy
  • 🎢 Roller coaster: P.E. ⇄ K.E. (+ internal energy and sound)
  • 🪀 Mass bouncing on a spring: K.E. ⇄ P.E. ⇄ elastic energy in the spring
Mechanical energyMechanical energy = P.E. + K.E.
If no energy is lost (no friction or air resistance):
P.E. + K.E. = constant
loss in P.E. = gain in K.E.
mgh = ½mv² (falling from rest)
Idea 2

Inter-conversion of P.E. and K.E.

No energy loss: total mechanical energy stays the same at every point.
Speed after falling a height h (from rest, no loss)mgh = ½mv² ⇒ v = √(2gh)
The mass cancels: heavy and light objects reach the same speed.
✏️ Ocean Park roller coaster (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A car starts from rest at the top and drops 40 m. Ignoring friction:
v = √(2 × 9.81 × 40) = 28.0 m s⁻¹ at the bottom.
Later it climbs a 25 m hump. Speed at the top of the hump: ½v² = 9.81 × (40 − 25) → v = 17.2 m s⁻¹.
Why conservation laws are powerfulOn a curved track the forces keep changing direction, so using Newton’s Second Law (F = ma) step by step would be very hard. With energy we only need the heights, not the shape of the track. The same idea works for a ball-bearing rolling down an inclined plane or a swinging pendulum.
Idea 3

Considering energy loss

In real life there is friction and air resistance. Mechanical energy is not conserved: some becomes internal energy (heat) and sound. The total energy is still conserved!

With energy lossinitial mechanical energy = final mechanical energy + energy lost
energy lost = work done against friction = f × s
✏️ A slide in a playground (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A 50 kg child starts from rest at the top of a slide 3.0 m high and 5.0 m long. Her speed at the bottom is 6.0 m s⁻¹.
  1. Loss in P.E. = mgh = 50 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 1471.5 J
  2. Gain in K.E. = ½ × 50 × 6.0² = 900 J
  3. Energy lost = 1471.5 − 900 = 571.5 J (≈ 572 J)
  4. Average friction = work done against friction ÷ distance = 571.5 ÷ 5.0 = 114 N
Common mistakes ⚠️Saying “energy is lost so it disappears”. It is not destroyed: it becomes internal energy of the slide, the child and the air. Also: friction acts over the length of the slide (5.0 m), not its height.

🧪 Experiment idea: roll a ball-bearing down an inclined plane and measure its speed at the bottom with a light gate. Compare ½mv² with mgh to find the energy lost.

Idea 4

Power: the rate of energy transfer

Definition ⭐P = W ÷ t = energy transferred ÷ time taken
Unit: watt (W), 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹; 1 kW = 1000 W
Power is a scalar.
Moving at constant velocityP = W/t = F s/t = F v
(F: driving force, v: velocity)
✏️ Your own power (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A 60 kg student runs up stairs 3.0 m high in 2.5 s.
Work done against gravity = mgh = 60 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 1766 J
P = 1766 ÷ 2.5 = 706 W
✏️ TruckA truck moves at a constant 15 m s⁻¹. The total resistive force is 3000 N, so the driving force is 3000 N.
P = Fv = 3000 × 15 = 45 000 W = 45 kW
🧪 Experiment: output power of an electric motorUse the motor to lift a mass m through a height h at steady speed. Time it with a stopwatch.
Output power = mgh ÷ t. e.g. 0.50 kg lifted 1.2 m in 4.0 s: P = 0.50 × 9.81 × 1.2 ÷ 4.0 = 1.47 W.
Measure the electrical input power too (V × I with meters) and compare: the output is always smaller.
Idea 5

Energy transfer devices in daily life

Efficiencyefficiency = useful energy output ÷ energy input × 100% = useful power output ÷ power input × 100%
No real device is 100% efficient: some energy always becomes internal energy (heat) and sound.
DeviceEnergy changeDesign idea using conservation of energy
🛗 Liftelectrical → P.E. (+ heat)A counterweight moves down as the car moves up, so the motor only supplies the difference in P.E.
🚶 Escalatorelectrical → P.E. of passengersPower needed = (number of people × mgh) ÷ time + losses; slows to standby when empty.
🚲 Bicyclechemical → K.E.Smooth bearings and pumped-up tyres reduce energy wasted against friction.
🔌 Kettle, fan, LED lampelectrical → internal energy / K.E. / lightPower rating (W) tells how fast energy is used; LEDs waste less as heat.
🚆 MTR / hybrid carK.E. → electrical (braking)Regenerative braking: the motor acts as a generator, so K.E. is stored instead of being wasted as heat.
🌏 Transport and the environmentFaster or heavier vehicles need more energy (K.E. = ½mv²) and more power to overcome air resistance. Using public transport, lighter cars, lower speeds and regenerative braking cuts energy use and pollution.
