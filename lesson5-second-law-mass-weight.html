Idea 1

What does a net force do?

A net force always causes an acceleration — a change of velocity. Velocity has size and direction, so a net force can change the speed, the direction, or both.

Direction of the net forceEffectExample
same direction as the motionspeeds up⚽ kicking a rolling ball forward; a minibus accelerating from a stop
opposite to the motionslows down (retardation (減速))🚗 braking; a ball rolling on grass
perpendicular (at 90°) to the motionchanges direction only (speed stays the same)🎡 a stone whirled on a string (more in Lesson 13)
at another anglechanges both speed and direction🏀 a basketball in flight: gravity is down, the ball moves up and sideways (Lesson 8)
RememberThe acceleration is always in the direction of the net force — not necessarily in the direction of the motion.
Idea 2

Newton’s Second Law (牛頓第二運動定律)

Newton’s Second LawIf a net force acts on an object, the object has an acceleration that is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass, in the direction of the net force.
Fnet = ma   (Fnet in N, m in kg, a in m s⁻²)
  • a ∝ Fnet (same mass): double the net force → double the acceleration.
  • a ∝ 1/m (same net force): double the mass → half the acceleration. (Bigger mass = bigger inertia, Lesson 3.)
  • Fnet = 0 → a = 0: this is Newton’s First Law again.
The newton (牛頓)1 N is the net force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m s⁻².
So 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻².
Isaac Newton published these laws in 1687; the unit of force is named after him.
✏️ Example + unit checkA 1200 kg car accelerates at 2.5 m s⁻². Find the net force.
  1. Fnet = ma = 1200 × 2.5 = 3000 N
  2. Unit check (dimension analysis (量綱分析)): kg × m s⁻² = kg m s⁻² = N ✓. If your units do not match, your formula is wrong!

Idea 3

Experiment: verifying F = ma

Set-up

  • A trolley on a friction-compensated runway (摩擦補償斜道): the runway is tilted a little until a trolley, once pushed, moves down with uniform velocity (evenly spaced ticker-tape dots / equal light-gate times). Then the weight component W sin θ balances friction, so the net force = the pulling force.
  • Pull the trolley with 1, 2, 3 identical elastic cords stretched to the same length (forces F, 2F, 3F), or with a force sensor.
  • Measure the acceleration with a ticker-tape timer, light gates or a motion sensor and a data logger.
NoteThe runway is not smooth: friction is just compensated. It only works for trolleys moving down the runway.

a against F (fixed mass): a straight line through the origin → a ∝ F.

a against 1/m (fixed force): a straight line through the origin → a ∝ 1/m. (a against m is a curve.) Stack 1, 2, 3 trolleys to change m.

Conclusiona ∝ F and a ∝ 1/m → a ∝ F/m. With the newton defined as above, the constant is 1: F = ma.

Idea 4

Mass (質量) and weight (重量)

Meaningamount of matter; a measure of inertiathe gravitational force on the object by the Earth (or planet)
Unitkilogram (kg)newton (N)

Changes from place to place?no — always the sameyes — e.g. smaller on the Moon

Relationship ⭐W = mg
In free fall the only force is W, so by Fnet = ma: W = m × g.
g = 9.81 m s⁻² = 9.81 N kg⁻¹ near the Earth’s surface (each kg weighs 9.81 N).
✏️ On Earth and on the MoonAn astronaut has a mass of 70 kg.
  1. Earth: W = 70 × 9.81 = 687 N
  2. Moon (g = 1.62 N kg⁻¹): W = 70 × 1.62 = 113 N (about ⅙)
  3. Mass on the Moon: still 70 kg.
⚠ Everyday languagePeople say “my weight is 50 kg”. In physics that is your mass. Your weight is 50 × 9.81 ≈ 491 N.
Idea 5

Free-body diagrams (自由體圖) and net force

How to draw a free-body diagram ⭐ ① Draw the object alone (a box or a dot). ② Draw every force acting on it, from where it acts, as an arrow. ③ Label each force (W, R, f, T, F…). Think: “force on the object by what?” ④ Do not draw forces the object exerts on other things.

📕 Book at rest: R = W, Fnet = 0.

📦 Pulled box: Fnet = 12 − 4 = 8 N right; vertically R = W.

⚾ Free fall: only W, so Fnet = W and a = g.

Net force from the diagramChoose a + direction. Add the forces along that line (opposite ones are negative). Perpendicular directions are treated separately; if there is no motion in a direction, the forces in that direction balance (e.g. R = W).
⚠ Common mistakes
  • Drawing a “force of motion” forward on a ball after it has been thrown. Once it leaves your hand, your push is gone!
  • A steel ball resting on the ground and just touching a wall: the wall pushes with no force (nothing presses it). Only W and R act.

Idea 6

Solving problems with Fnet = ma

Method ⭐① Free-body diagram. ② Choose + direction (usually the direction of motion). ③ Write Fnet = (sum of forces) = ma. ④ Solve. ⑤ Units and a sense check.
✏️ A box pushed with 10 N (m = 2 kg)
  1. Smooth floor (no friction): a = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 m s⁻²
  2. Maximum friction 5 N: it slides; Fnet = 10 − 5 = 5 N → a = 2.5 m s⁻²
  3. Maximum friction 15 N: 10 N is less than 15 N → it does not move; friction = 10 N, a = 0
✏️ Finding frictionA 3 kg block is pulled with 7 N and accelerates at 2 m s⁻².
  1. Fnet = ma: 7 − f = 3 × 2
  2. f = 7 − 6 = 1 N
✏️ Braking carA 1500 kg car slows from 20 m s⁻¹ to rest in 4 s.
  1. a = (0 − 20) ÷ 4 = −5 m s⁻²
  2. Fnet = ma = 1500 × (−5) = −7500 N (7500 N backward)
✏️ Smooth slope (g = 9.81 m s⁻²)A block slides down a smooth slope at 30°.
  1. Net force along the slope = mg sin 30°
  2. ma = mg sin 30° → a = g sin 30° = 4.91 m s⁻² (the mass cancels!)
Idea 7

In a lift 🛗: apparent weight (視重)

You do not feel your weight directly. You feel the normal reaction R from the floor (or what a bathroom scale reads). Your weight W = mg does not change in a lift, but R does. Take up = +:

Fnet = ma for a person in a liftR − mg = ma → R = m(g + a)
Lift motionAcceleration aRYou feel
at rest or uniform velocity (up or down)0R = mgnormal
moving up and speeding up / moving down and slowing downupward (+)R > mgheavier
moving down and speeding up / moving up and slowing downdownward (−)R < mglighter
falling freely (cable broken!)−gR = 0weightless (失重)
✏️ Example (g = 10 m s⁻²)John (80 kg) stands on a scale in a lift. The scale reads 600 N.
  1. W = mg = 800 N (unchanged!)
  2. R − mg = ma → 600 − 800 = 80a
  3. a = −2.5 m s⁻² → 2.5 m s⁻² downward (moving down and speeding up, or moving up and slowing down)
